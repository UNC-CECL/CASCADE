Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`coastsat_projected_vs_duneline_1996_2010_2024_halves_overlay.png`.** The 1996–2024 CoastSat linear regression rate × 14 yr — one long-term trend, PROJECTED onto each half — against the dune line's measured net change over that same half, by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island). The coloured line and fill are the shoreline (blue seaward, red landward) with its single transects as dots; the black line is the dune line. **The shoreline side is identical in the two panels** — same rate, same 14 yr — so every difference between them is the dune line's, which is what this figure is for. Where the black line sits below the fill's edge the beach widened. The dune line spans its own two photographs, 11.6 yr in the first half and 14.1 yr in the second (the 2023 date assumed), against the shoreline's 14 calendar years in both, so each gap also holds that much shoreline drift. Per window: 1996–2010 shoreline +2.1 m, dune line -16.2 m, beach width +18.3 m, r = 0.37; 2010–2024 shoreline +2.1 m, dune line +1.4 m, beach width +0.8 m, r = 0.85. Seaward positive, in metres; the y axis is ±100 m on both panels, the fixed metre axis. Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers.

# Captions — 1996_2024

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`lrr_1996_2024.png`.** Observed shoreline change rate by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island), 1996–2024: for each CoastSat transect the ordinary-least-squares slope of shoreline position against date over the calendar window (1 January 1996 to 31 December 2024, at least 3 positions), seaward positive. The coloured line and fill are the mean of the ~10 transects in each 500 m domain, blue where the shoreline moved seaward and red where it moved landward; the dotted lines are ±1 standard deviation across them; the dots are the single transects, blue or red by their own sign. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±8 m/yr, the bound of every window figure (the largest |domain mean| over all windows plus 1 m, rounded up). This window is context: no model run is graded against it.

**`smoothing_windows_1996_2024.png`.** Observed shoreline change rate by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island), 1996–2024, at the three alongshore smoothing widths. For each CoastSat transect the rate is the ordinary-least-squares slope of shoreline position against date over the calendar window (1 January 1996 to 31 December 2024), seaward positive. The palest line with markers is the mean of the ~10 transects in each 500 m domain, unsmoothed; the three heavier curves are a LOESS fitted to the transects at 1.5 km, 2.5 km and 5 km, light to dark, the darkest being the 10-domain window every run is graded at (1.5 km frac 0.033; 2.5 km frac 0.055; 5 km frac 0.110). Every curve keeps the raw domain means over GIS 1–10 — the boundary treatment at Oregon Inlet — so all four are identical there by construction. Each window takes this much alongshore structure out of the field, as the standard deviation of what it removes: 1.5 km 0.140; 2.5 km 0.244; 5 km 0.573 m/yr, against a domain-mean standard deviation of 1.383 m/yr. Widening the window is not denoising this field: the scatter between transects inside one domain is only 0.225 m/yr and is larger than the median per-transect regression uncertainty (0.151 m/yr), so it is not clean estimation noise, and the domain-mean rate decorrelates alongshore at about 1.5 km, three times narrower than the 5 km window. What that window removes is concentrated in the two shoal-fronted peaks (GIS 29–35, GIS 65–72), which hold 42% of the removed signal by squared amplitude on 15 of the 80 smoothed domains. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±8 m/yr, the bound of every window figure. This window is context: no model run is graded against it.

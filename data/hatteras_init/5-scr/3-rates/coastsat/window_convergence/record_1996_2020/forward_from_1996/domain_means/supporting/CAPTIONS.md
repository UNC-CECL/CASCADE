# Captions — domain_means

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`window_convergence_domains_forward_from_1996.png`.** How wrong a window's rate is, and which windows stop being wrong. Each panel is one domain mean's fitted rate MINUS its own 1996–2020 rate, against the moving year of the window (start pinned at 1996, end moving); zero is agreement and all eight panels share one axis, so a panel compares with a panel. The blue funnel is the headline tolerance, CI overlap: a window passes where its own 95% interval reaches the reference's, so the band is wide where the record is short and narrows onto the reference at the full length. The purple band inside it is the reference fit's own 95% half-width, the strictest tolerance, drawn for scale; the other five are in tolerance_comparison_forward_from_1996.png. The green dash is the convergence window — the shortest window after which the curve never leaves the funnel again, median 19 years of record, and each panel title names its own. The amber point is 1996–2010, the marked window. The windows are nested, so the curve reaching zero at 1996–2020 is structural: read where it enters the band for good, not the fact of arrival. Beyond ±4 m/yr, off the axis and marked with a triangle at the edge: -10.8 m/yr at GIS 1 (2000); -9.4 m/yr at GIS 77 (2000).

**`domain_mean_vs_transect_forward_from_1996.png`.** What averaging a domain buys. The model is graded on the domain MEAN of its ~10 transect rates, so a single transect's convergence window is an upper bound; blue is the mean, grey the median over the same domain's individual transects. (a) years of record the rate needs before its own 95% interval reaches the 1996–2020 fit's for good — CI overlap, the headline tolerance — against the 15 years the 1996–2010 window has (amber). (b) that window's error against the 1996–2020 rate, with ±0.25 m/yr as hairlines. Averaging moves the island median from 20 years to 20, and 30 of 90 domain means have their 1996–2010 rate inside the band. The mean's band is the MEAN of the transects' 95% half-widths, not a standard error of the mean: adjacent transects are views of the same shoreline and propagating them as independent would divide the band by about √10 and manufacture a later window out of an assumption. Thin grey verticals are the eight sites of the panel figures.

**`tolerance_comparison_forward_from_1996.png`.** What “close enough” costs. Every tolerance the sweep scores, drawn on the same error curve. (a) and (b) are single transects: the black line is the window’s rate minus the 1996–2020 rate, the dashed pairs are the fixed tolerances, and the blue funnel is CI overlap — the only one that is not a band, because it widens with the window’s own uncertainty and narrows onto the reference. The triangles on the zero line mark where each tolerance says the rate has settled. (a) is the transect where the four disagree most, (b) one where they nearly agree. (c) the whole island: the share of the 90 transects settled by a given record length, one curve per tolerance, with the marked window’s 15 years dotted — the horizontal gap between curves is what the choice of tolerance is worth, in years. Island medians: the reference fit's 95% band 1996–2018 (11% of marked windows pass), 3× the reference band 1996–2015 (30% of marked windows pass), ±0.50 m/yr 1996–2015 (26% of marked windows pass), CI overlap 1996–2015 (33% of marked windows pass), ±1.00 m/yr 1996–2011 (53% of marked windows pass). The windows are nested, so every curve reaches 100% at the full record by construction. 3× the reference band and ±0.50 m/yr are nearly the same width on this record — the reference fit’s half-width is about 0.15 m/yr — so their curves in (c) track each other, and the choice between them is not worth arguing about.

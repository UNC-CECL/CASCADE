# Captions — sites

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`shoreline_position_window_fits_backward_from_2020.png`.** The record the sweep is fitted on. Every CoastSat shoreline position at one transect in each of eight evenly spaced domains, 1996 to 2020, with the annual median through them and six of the 21 nested fits drawn as the straight lines they are. Every window ends 2020; each line spans the window it was fitted on and is labelled with its moving year. Amber is 2010–2020, the marked window; purple is 1996–2020, the reference every window converges on. Where the lines separate, the rate still depends on where the window ends. The intermediate windows are fitted and tabled but not drawn. Position is CoastSat chainage, seaward positive, on an origin that is arbitrary per transect: only the SLOPE compares between panels, and each panel is autoscaled. The widest disagreement is GIS 1, where the marked window gives -3.92 m/yr against the reference's +0.21.

**`window_convergence_backward_from_2020.png`.** How wrong a window's rate is, and which windows stop being wrong. Each panel is one transect's fitted rate MINUS its own 1996–2020 rate, against the moving year of the window (end pinned at 2020, start moving); zero is agreement and all eight panels share one axis, so a panel compares with a panel. The blue funnel is the headline tolerance, CI overlap: a window passes where its own 95% interval reaches the reference's, so the band is wide where the record is short and narrows onto the reference at the full length. The purple band inside it is the reference fit's own 95% half-width, the strictest tolerance, drawn for scale; the other five are in tolerance_comparison_backward_from_2020.png. The green dash is the convergence window — the shortest window after which the curve never leaves the funnel again, median 18 years of record, and each panel title names its own. The amber point is 2010–2020, the marked window. The windows are nested, so the curve reaching zero at 1996–2020 is structural: read where it enters the band for good, not the fact of arrival. Beyond ±4 m/yr, off the axis and marked with a triangle at the edge: -4.3 m/yr at GIS 1 (2008); +8.3 m/yr at GIS 13 (2016); -9.6 m/yr at GIS 77 (2016); -5.2 m/yr at GIS 90 (2015).

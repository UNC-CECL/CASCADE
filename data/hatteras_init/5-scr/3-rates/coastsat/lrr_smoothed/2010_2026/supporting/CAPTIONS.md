# Captions — 2010_2026

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`lrr_lowess5_vs_lowess7_2010_2026.png`.** Observed shoreline change rate by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island), 2010–2026, smoothed alongshore at two widths. For each CoastSat transect the rate is the ordinary-least-squares slope of shoreline position against date over the calendar window (1 January 2010 to 31 December 2026), seaward positive; the grey dots are those transect rates (2 beyond the axis, drawn as open circles at its edge). The palest line with markers is the mean of the ~10 transects in each 500 m domain, unsmoothed. The two heavier curves are a LOWESS fitted to the transects at 2.5 km (5 domains, frac 0.055) and 3.5 km (7 domains, frac 0.077), the darker being the 7-domain width every run is graded at. Both keep the raw domain means over GIS 1–10, the boundary treatment at Oregon Inlet, so they are identical there by construction. North of GIS 10 the 7-domain curve differs from the 5-domain one by 0.189 m/yr (standard deviation over 80 domains), at most 0.59 m/yr at GIS 66, and by more than 0.5 m/yr at 2 domains. The shaded band on the 7-domain curve is the 95% confidence interval of the transect fits, smoothed the same way (the half-widths are averaged, not combined as independent errors, because neighbouring transects are not independent). The dashed grey line and band are the long-term 1996–2026 rate, fitted and smoothed the same way. North of GIS 10 this window's 7-domain curve sits 0.89 m/yr above it on average (root mean square difference 1.22 m/yr), and the two bands do not overlap at 55 of 80 domains (GIS 11–28, 33–40, 46–47, 50–52, 60–68, 70, 77–90). Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±12 m/yr, shared by every figure of the candidate windows.

**`lrr_lowess7_2010_2020_and_2010_2026_vs_1996_2026.png`.** Observed shoreline change rate by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island) for 2010–2020 (purple) and 2010–2026 (dark blue), the same start with the record cut before and after the 2021 shoreline step, over the long-term 1996–2026 rate (dashed grey). Each curve is the per-transect CoastSat linear regression rate over the calendar window, LOWESS-smoothed at 3.5 km (7 domains) with the raw domain means kept over GIS 1–10; each band is the 95% confidence interval of the transect fits, smoothed the same way. North of GIS 10, 2010–2020 sits 0.25 m/yr below the long-term rate on average (bands apart at 46 of 80 domains) and 2010–2026 sits 0.89 m/yr above it (bands apart at 55); 2010–2026 sits 1.14 m/yr above 2010–2020 (root mean square difference 1.61 m/yr, bands apart at 46 domains: GIS 12–28, 37–47, 52–69). What separates the two windows comes from 2021–2026, the years they do not share. Black bars above the frame mark the fills inside 2010–2026 at the hindcast footprint. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±12 m/yr, shared by every figure of the candidate windows.

# Captions — 2010_2024

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`projected_2010_2024.png`.** Projected shoreline change by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island), 2010–2024: for each CoastSat transect the 1996–2024 linear regression rate (the ordinary-least-squares slope through every satellite position from 1 January 1996 to 31 December 2024) multiplied by 14 yr, the distance the shoreline would have moved at that rate. The rate is fitted on 1996–2024 but evaluated over 2010–2024, a window it was not fitted on, which is what makes this a PROJECTION: it is the long-term trend asked what 2010–2024 should have looked like. The coloured line and fill are the mean of the ~10 transects in each 500 m domain, blue seaward and red landward, so the alongshore pattern is the 1996–2024 rate's; the dots are the single transects, blue or red by their own sign. The PURPLE line is the OBSERVED ENDPOINT change over the same 14 yr: per transect the mean position over all of 2024 minus the mean over all of 2010, averaged per domain — no rate anywhere in it. Where it lies above the fill the shoreline ended more seaward than the trend implies; below, more landward. Over the 90 domains with both, observed minus projected averages +9.3 m (range -27.5 to +84.9 m). Seaward positive, in metres. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±100 m, fixed, the same on every metre figure in 3-rates, 4-comparisons and target_comparison. Beyond ±100 m, off the axis and marked with a triangle at the edge: the observed change +125 m at GIS 1.

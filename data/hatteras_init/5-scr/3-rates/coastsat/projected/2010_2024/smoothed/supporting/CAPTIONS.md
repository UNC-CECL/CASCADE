# Captions — smoothed

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`projected_smoothed_2010_2024_w03.png`.** Projected shoreline change and observed change, 2010–2024, both passed through the same alongshore LOESS of 3 domains (1.5 km). The quantity the model is graded against is not the raw rate but this one — raw domain means over GIS 1–10 and a LOESS of the transect values beyond — so this panel tests the 1996–2024 trend as it is actually applied, not as it is estimated. PROJECTED SHORELINE CHANGE (coloured line and fill, blue seaward and red landward) is each transect's 1996–2024 linear regression rate × 14 yr, smoothed alongshore at transect resolution and then averaged per 500 m domain — the rate is fitted on 1996–2024 and carried onto 2010–2024, a window it was not fitted on. OBSERVED (purple line) is the mean CoastSat position over all of 2024 minus the mean over all of 2010, per transect, through the identical pass — both sides are smoothed, so the gap between them is not an artefact of treating them differently. The dots are the RAW individual transects, unsmoothed, drawn so the width of the cloud the line was drawn through stays visible. Over 90 domains the residual (observed minus projected) has mean +10.0 m and RMS 17.9 m, against +9.3 m and 19.4 m unsmoothed; it ranges -19.1 to +84.9 m. What survives this window is a place the 1996–2024 rate genuinely fails, at the scale the model resolves; what disappears between windows was transect-scale scatter. The correlation is deliberately not quoted: a symmetric smoother removes variance that is uncorrelated between the two sides, so r climbs with the window whether or not the smoothing is telling the truth (it is in tables/residual_by_scale.csv, named for that). Seaward positive, in metres. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±100 m, fixed, the same on every metre figure. Beyond ±100 m, off the axis and marked with a triangle at the edge: the smoothed observed change +125 m at GIS 1.

**`projected_smoothed_2010_2024_overlay.png`.** This window is the test period (held out) of the 1996–2010–2024 chain. Projected shoreline change by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island), 2010–2024, at each alongshore smoothing width. Each transect's 1996–2024 linear regression rate × 14 yr is the distance the shoreline would have moved at that rate, the rate being fitted on 1996–2024 and carried onto 2010–2024; the palest line with markers is the mean of those over the ~10 transects in each 500 m domain, unsmoothed, and the three heavier curves are the same quantity after the LOESS the model target is built through, at 1.5 km, 2.5 km and 5 km, light to dark, the darkest being the 10-domain window every run is graded at. Every curve keeps the raw domain means over GIS 1–10 — the boundary treatment at Oregon Inlet — so all four are identical there by construction. The observed endpoint change is deliberately not drawn: this figure is about what the window does to the target, and the observed comparison is the three panels beside it. Smoothing moves the projection by 1.5 km up to 7 m; 2.5 km up to 10 m; 5 km up to 24 m at the most affected domain, against a raw domain-mean range of -35 to +44 m. This is the rate figure `3-rates/coastsat/lrr/1996_2024/smoothing_windows_1996_2024.png` in metres — a LOESS commutes with the × 14 yr multiply, so the curves have the same shape and only the units differ. Seaward positive, in metres. Black bars above the panel mark the beach fills placed inside the window, at the footprint the hindcast uses (2014 at GIS 84–89; 2022 at GIS 6–15; 2022 at GIS 21–28). Hatched amber boxes mark the offshore shoals (Avon Shoals GIS 24–39; Wimble Shoals GIS 60–74). Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is ±100 m, fixed, so the 28 yr figure reads against the two 14 yr ones and against every other metre figure. It is the fixed metre axis every figure in this tree uses.

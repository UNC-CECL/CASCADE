# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`stable_dune_growth_rate_1996_2009.png`.** Growth rate from stable dunes, 1996 start, Dmax 10.5 m above the berm (Dmaxel 12.2 m NAVD88), groin on. Model median dune crest on 1 January 2010 minus the 2009 lidar median on the same 10 m cells, for two stretches with a stable shoreline (CoastSat 1996-2009 LRR within +/-0.5 m/yr), no observed overwash in 1996-2009 and dunes present and changing gently in all nine surveys: GIS 59-62 (Mid-island / Wimble Shoals) and GIS 20-22 (Buxton-Avon / Avon). Dotted: the seven domains together. Rates are rmin-rmax ranges plotted at their mean. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_growth_rate.py.

**`stable_dune_fig1_where.png`.** Where the dunes were stable, 1996-2009. (a) Shoreline change rate per domain (CoastSat linear regression, 1996-2009); dark bars lie within +/-0.5 m/yr. (b) Change in the domain-median dune crest between the 1996 and 2009 lidar surveys, on the 10 m cells with a dune in all nine surveys. (c) Number of imagery dates showing overwash. Shaded: GIS 20-22 (Buxton-Avon / Avon) and GIS 59-62 (Mid-island / Wimble Shoals), where the shoreline held, no overwash was seen and the dune changed gently; these calibrate the growth rate. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig2_profiles.png`.** Cross-shore profiles at the two stable stretches from the 1996 and 2009 lidar DEMs, in the same frame (the 1996 start's, 10 m cells; line: median of the domain's 50 alongshore rows, band: 25th-75th percentile; only cells covered by that year's own survey). Grey: the dune window the model reads its dune from. Shoreline and dune stayed in place and the crest rose slightly: a dune that grew without storm loss or shoreline change. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig3_excluded.png`.** As stable_dune_fig2 at two domains left out of the calibration. GIS 44: the beach built out (+1.7 m/yr) and the dune grew 2 m, fed by the extra sand, which would overstate r. GIS 65: the shoreline retreated (-2.2 m/yr); the crest held while the dune lost width, which r cannot represent. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig4_surveys.png`.** Dune crests at the stable stretches through all nine lidar surveys, 1996-2009 (median of the 10 m cells with a dune in every survey; thin lines: single domains, labelled). The crests sit above Isabel's run-up and change gradually, with no drop after Isabel (2004 survey), so the change between surveys is growth rather than storm damage: the process r controls. 1996-2005 surveys are 3-9 m grids, 2009 the 1 m DEM. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig5_model.png`.** Model against the surveys at the stable stretches, 1996 start, Dmax 10.5 m above the berm, groin on. Median crest of the cells with a dune in every survey, for four growth rates; circles: the lidar on the same cells, at the model state each survey's flight dates point to. With no growth the model dunes stay flat while the real ones rose; 0.02 follows the surveys on the corrected 1996 start (ALACE -0.24 m); faster rates outgrow them. The first-year rise at every rate, including no growth, is the roadway manager bulldozing 3,400-5,100 m3 of sand above its road elevation onto the dunes once, in the road-managed domains (GIS 20 and 59-62; 21-22 are a community zone without it). Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

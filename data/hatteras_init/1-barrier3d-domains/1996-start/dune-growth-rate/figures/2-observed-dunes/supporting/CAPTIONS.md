# Captions — 2-observed-dunes

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`stable_dune_fig2_profiles.png`.** Cross-shore profiles at the two stable stretches from the 1996 and 2009 lidar DEMs, in the same frame (the 1996 start's, 10 m cells; line: median of the domain's 50 alongshore rows, band: 25th-75th percentile; only cells covered by that year's own survey). Grey: the dune window the model reads its dune from. Shoreline and dune stayed in place and the crest rose slightly: a dune that grew without storm loss or shoreline change. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig4_surveys.png`.** Dune crests at the stable stretches through all nine lidar surveys, 1996-2009 (median of the 10 m cells with a dune in every survey; thin lines: single domains, labelled). The crests sit above Isabel's run-up and change gradually, with no drop after Isabel (2004 survey), so the change between surveys is growth rather than storm damage: the process r controls. 1996-2005 surveys are 3-9 m grids, 2009 the 1 m DEM. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig7_dem_gis20.png`.** The same dune in both surveys, GIS 20. (a) The 1996 lidar (ALACE, lowered 0.24 m) and (b) the 2009 lidar (USACE) at 1 m, north up, true scale, over a hillshade; grey where that year's own survey has no data (the 1996 start is the 2009 DEM there, so it is not shown). (c) 2009 minus 1996: blue gain, red loss. Dashed: the model's dune window, the band of 10 m cells each row's crest is searched in. Dots: the crest picked in each 10 m alongshore row from that year's DEM, averaged to 10 m as the model sees it. The picks follow one ridge in both years: 76% of the 50 rows picked in both years are within one 10 m cell of each other. The white blocks landward of the dune in (a) are houses: the 1996 ALACE grid keeps buildings that the 2009 bare-earth DEM removes, hence the red patches in (c); they sit outside the dune window. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig8_alongshore.png`.** Dune crest along the stable stretches, 1996-2009, one value per 10 m model row (the highest 10 m cell in the model's dune window). Red and amber: the 1 m 1996 and 2009 DEMs the model's dunes and its 2009 target come from; grey: the 1997-2005 surveys, coarser grids. The 1996 and 2009 crests rise and dip at the same places alongshore, so the window picks the same dune in both surveys, and the 2009 line sits a little above the 1996 one almost everywhere: the dune grew in place. Gaps: rows a survey did not cover. The two single-row 1996 spikes in GIS 21 and 22 (above 9 m) appear in no other survey. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fig9_crest_shift.png`.** How far the picked dune crest moved cross-shore between the 1996 and 2009 lidar, per 10 m row at the seven stable domains (rows picked in both years). Positions are 10 m model cells, so 0 means the same cell. 87% of rows are within one cell: the crest picked in 2009 is the 1996 dune, not a different ridge. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

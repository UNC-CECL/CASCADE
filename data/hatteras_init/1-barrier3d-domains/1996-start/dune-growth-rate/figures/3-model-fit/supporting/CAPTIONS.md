# Captions — 3-model-fit

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`stable_dune_fit_trajectory.png`.** The adopted growth rate follows the stable dunes through 1996-2009. Model crests with r 0.01-0.03 (line: median; band: middle half of the cells) against every lidar survey on the same cells (circles: median; bars: middle half), at the model state each survey's flight dates point to. 1996 start corrected for the ALACE offset, Dmax 10.5 m above the berm, groin on. Dotted: no growth; dashed: r = 0.04. 1996-2005 surveys are 3-9 m grids, which read sharp crests somewhat low. The first-year rise in every run, no growth included, is the roadway manager bulldozing 3,400-5,100 m3 of sand above its road elevation onto the dunes once, in the road-managed domains (GIS 20 and 59-62; 21-22 are a community zone without it). Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fit_error_by_rate.png`.** Every growth rate tested on the stable dunes, 1996 start (ALACE offset corrected), Dmax 10.5 m above the berm, groin on, ordered by mean rate. (a) Model minus lidar median crest at the 2009 survey, per stretch and for the seven domains together; 0 matches the lidar. (b) RMSE of the median crest over the surveys after 1996. Numbers from tables/stable_dune_summary.csv. Rates averaging 0.02 (0.02, 0.01-0.03, 0.005-0.035) are the ones near zero at both stretches with the lowest RMSE; slower rates leave the dunes too low, faster ones too high. Shaded: the adopted range. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

**`stable_dune_fit_spread.png`.** Why a range of growth rates rather than one: crest change per 10 m cell at the seven stable domains, 1996 to 2009 (346 cells with a crest in every survey). Shape: distribution; bar: middle half; circle: median; dashed: the observed median. All three model rates match the observed median, but a fixed 0.02 grows every cell alike. 0.01-0.03 doubles the cell-to-cell spread at the same median; 0.005-0.035 widens it further but lowers the median. Barrier3D draws each cell's r at random within the range, so the spread is not placed where the real fast and slow dunes are. Producer: HAT_stable_dune_figures.py.

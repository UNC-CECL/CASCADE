# Captions — model-fit

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`test_growth_fit_trajectory.png`.** The adopted test-period growth rate against the 2014-2019 lidar, island-wide (4500 dune cells with a crest in every survey). (a) Median crest; line and band: the model with r 0.02-0.04 (median, middle half of the cells); circles and bars: the lidar. (b) The 10th-percentile crest, the low dunes. Square: the 2009 lidar, which is the model's start. Dotted: the calibration rate, 0.01-0.03; dashed: 0.03-0.07. 2009 start, Dmaxel 12.2 m, groin on, ends re-solved 2026-10-20. The rate is fitted to these surveys, so this window tests overwash and shoreline, not dune growth. Producer: HAT_test_period_growth_figures.py.

**`test_growth_fit_error_by_rate.png`.** Every growth rate tested on the 2009 start against the 2014-2019 lidar, island-wide, ordered by mean rate. (a) Model minus lidar at the 2019 survey: median crest (filled) and the low dunes' 10th-percentile crest (open). (b) RMSE over the five surveys. 0.02-0.04 has the median closest to the lidar and the lowest median RMSE; the calibration rate falls behind, faster rates overshoot. The low dunes fit the calibration rate better (2019 error -0.13 vs +0.51 m, RMSE 0.36 vs 0.45 m): the adopted rate builds them too high. Same numbers as tables/test_period_growth_scores.csv; each survey scored on the cells it covers. Producer: HAT_test_period_growth_figures.py.

**`test_growth_fit_spread.png`.** Crest change per 10 m cell, island-wide, from the 2009 lidar (the model's start) to the 2019 survey (4500 cells with a crest in every survey). Shape: distribution; bar: middle half; circle: median; dashed: the observed median. 0.02-0.04 comes closest to the observed median; every rate gives less spread than the lidar. Cells that lost their dune to overwash sit in the left tail. Barrier3D draws each cell's r at random within the range, so the model's spread is not placed where the real fast and slow dunes are. Producer: HAT_test_period_growth_figures.py.

# Captions — domains

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`offset_1996_duneline_vs_shoreline_domains_GIS01-15.png`.** GIS 1–15 of the 1996 start island as the model builds it from each offset source, one of 6 figures covering GIS 1–90. Every Barrier3D domain of the 1996 start topography (1984-start, dune-topo v2) is drawn at its island offset: (a) with the dune-line build (duneline/v1), (b) with the shoreline build (shoreline/v2), on the same axes. The grids are identical in both panels; only where each domain sits cross-shore changes. Each column of cells is one 500 m domain: its seaward edge is the BRIE shoreline, then the model's 30 m initial beach, the two dune rows and the interior (water masked); landward is up. The dash across each domain in (a) and (b) is where its shoreline WOULD be with the other source: white, over the ocean, where the other source puts the domain further seaward; black, over the beach, where it puts it further landward. (c) gives that move in metres per domain, from (a) to (b): blue up is landward, red down is seaward; the shoreline offset places 3 of 15 domains here further seaward. Grey strips along the bottom of (a) and (b) mark the villages. Offsets are in the model frame, each build zeroed on its own most seaward domain (GIS 77 for the dune line, GIS 76 for the shoreline), which is what Cascade receives up to one shared constant (checked against a run's year-0 positions to within 1 m). Because of that zeroing the shift in (c) is not the distance from shoreline to dune line, which is always seaward (beach width 16–96 m): it is 39.3 m, the gap between the two zero points, minus the beach width at the domain. A domain with a beach wider than 39 m moves seaward under the shoreline offset, a narrower one landward. Vertical exaggeration is large.

**`offset_1996_duneline_vs_shoreline_domains_overview.png`.** The 1996 island offset from each source over the whole island, the overview for the 6 section figures beside it (shaded bands, named along the bottom). The cross-shore position each offset gives every 500 m domain: dune line digitised from 1997 aerial imagery (duneline/v1) and mean satellite shoreline, Oct 1995 – Oct 1997 (CoastSat) (shoreline/v2), each in the model frame, zeroed on its own most seaward domain (GIS 77 and GIS 76); landward is up. At this scale the two lines overlap: they differ by tens of metres on a planform that spans about 6 km, which is why the section figures zoom to 15 domains and give the shift per domain.

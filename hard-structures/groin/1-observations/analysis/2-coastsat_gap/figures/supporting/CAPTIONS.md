# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`coastsat_gap_1_transect_map.png`.** **Tests:** which CoastSat transects define each of the two gaps used in this analysis. **How to read:** UTM 18N, north up, ocean to the right; each coloured line is one CoastSat transect, blue on the north (updrift) side of the groins and gold on the south (downdrift) side; red bars are the four groins of the Buxton field; grey boxes are the model domains. (a) The domain gap, the quantity the model is fitted on: every transect in GIS 6 (6) and GIS 5 (13), the two domains the model's groin sits between. (b) The near-field gap, a check: transects whose landward end lies within 500 m north of the northernmost groin (4) or south of the southernmost (12). (c) Locator: Hatteras Island, with the extent of (a) and (b) in teal. The black line is the CoastSat mean shoreline over the 1996 DEM window; the arrow is the direction of the wave-climate net longshore drift at Buxton (southward), which makes the north side updrift. **Shows:** the southernmost GIS 6 transect lies just south of the southern groin, so the domain gap is not purely across the field; the near-field gap has no such transect and gives the same break year (figure 5). The fourth groin line is the landward anti-flanking extension of the northern groin.

**`coastsat_gap_2_one_transect.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this shows the first step of the analysis on one transect. **How to read:** (a) Grey dots: the shoreline position CoastSat found on transect usa_NC_0032_0070 (the middle of the 13 GIS 5 transects) in each satellite image, as distance along the transect, seaward positive. Gold: the calendar-year mean, kept where at least 3 images fall in the year (hollow: fewer, not used). Dashed: the transect's own mean over 1984-2016; every later figure uses each annual mean minus this value (an anomaly), so transects with different starting positions can be averaged. (b) The number of images behind each annual mean. Grey shading: after the 2017 fill, left out of every fit. **Shows:** a single image scatters tens of metres about its year's mean; the yearly mean averages that out, from 4 to 31 images a year over 1984-2016, fewest before 1999.

**`coastsat_gap_3_transects_to_sides.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this shows the second step, from transects to one position per side. **How to read:** thin lines: each transect's annual anomaly (figure 2) in GIS 6 (a, north, updrift) and GIS 5 (b, south, downdrift); thick line: the side's position, the mean of its transects in each year where at least 50% of them have an annual mean. Dotted lines: Hurricane Gordon (1994), the last repair (1995), Hurricane Isabel (2003), the Buxton fill (2017); grey: after the fill, not fitted. **Shows:** before the fill the transects on a side move together, so the mean is a fair summary of the side. After it one GIS 6 line parts from the rest and rises with GIS 5: transect 0077, the southernmost GIS 6 transect, which lies just south of the southern groin (figure 1) and so takes the fill sand moving south past the field. The next figure puts the two side means together.

**`coastsat_gap_4_side_positions.png`.** **Tests:** whether a change in the gap comes from the north side holding (trapping) or from the south side moving. **How to read:** CoastSat annual shoreline position of each side, seaward positive, each transect taken about its own 1984-2016 mean and averaged per side; the gap in figure 5 is blue minus gold. Dotted lines: Hurricane Gordon (1994), the last repair (1995), Hurricane Isabel (2003), the Buxton fill (2017); grey: years after the fill, left out of every fit. (a) Whole domains, GIS 6 and GIS 5. (b) Transects within 500 m of the groin field. **Shows:** the 1991-1995 widening of the gap is mostly the south side retreating (about 98 m in GIS 5 from 1984 to 1995, against about 24 m on the north side), with Gordon in that stretch, so the 1995 peak is partly storm loss downdrift rather than trapping. After 2017 the south side gains as the fill sand moves south past the groins.

**`coastsat_gap_5_gap_and_break.png`.** **Tests:** in which year the groin stopped holding the north side ahead of the south, i.e. when the gap stopped widening. **How to read:** The gap is the north (updrift) shoreline position minus the south (downdrift) one, seaward positive, so a rising gap means the north side is holding while the south side retreats, which is what a working groin does. CoastSat values are calendar-year means per transect (at least 3 images), each taken about its own 1984-2016 mean and averaged per side in years where at least half the side's transects have data. Pale thick line: a continuous line with one change of slope (a hinge), fitted on 1984-2016; dashed line: the year of that change, with its 90% range from 2000 residual bootstraps shaded. Grey: after the 2017 fill, not fitted. (a) Whole domains: +4.1 m/yr to 1995, then -1.4 m/yr. (b) Within 500 m of the field: +3.3 then -2.4 m/yr, break 1995. **Shows:** both gaps stop widening in 1995, the year of the last repair, not at Hurricane Isabel (2003); what follows is a slow decline, not a collapse. The photo gap on the same axes is in `../3-photos_vs_coastsat/`.

**`coastsat_gap_6_break_year.png`.** **Tests:** how sharply the data pick out the break year, and whether the timing the model assumes (a failure around 2004) is compatible with them. **How to read:** (a) For every candidate break year, the misfit (sum of squared residuals) of the one-break fit to the 1984-2016 annual gap, divided by the misfit of the best year, so 1 is the best fit and higher is worse. (b) The best break year in each of 2000 residual bootstraps, as a share of all bootstraps. Grey band: 2002-2005, around the model's 2004 failure step. **Shows:** both gaps fit best with a 1995 break; 2002-2005 holds 1% (whole domains) and 2% (near field) of the bootstraps, while 1997 or earlier holds 91% and 86%.

**`coastsat_gap_7_era_rates.png`.** **Tests:** whether the gap widened (groin trapping), held, or narrowed in each era of the structure's history. **How to read:** (a) The whole-domain annual gap (figure 5) with a least-squares line through the years of each era: up to the 1995 last repair, from 1996 to Hurricane Isabel (2003), and from 2004 to the 2017 fill. (b) Each line's slope ± 1 standard error, for both gap definitions. **Shows:** both gaps widened before the repair. From 1996 to 2003 they narrowed slightly, not distinguishably from zero, and from 2004 to the fill they were flat. The photo rates on the same eras are compared in `../3-photos_vs_coastsat/`.

**`coastsat_gap_8_robustness.png`.** **Tests:** whether the 1995 break depends on choices in the fit, and whether the data also need a sudden drop at 2004, the year the model's groin fails. **How to read:** each row is one version of the fit: the baseline (1984-2016); starting in 1988, which drops the sparse early Landsat years (5-10 images a year); leaving out the 1995 peak; ending in 2013. (a) The best break year with its 90% range from 500 residual bootstraps; grey: 2002-2005. (b) The size of a level step at 2004 fitted alongside the free break, ± 1 standard error; a step that matters would sit clearly away from zero. **Shows:** the break stays at 1994-1996 in every version. The step is negative but within two standard errors of zero in all eight fits, and adding it lowers the BIC below the break-only fit in only one (whole domains with 1995 left out), by 0.1 (tables/gap_robustness.csv).

**`coastsat_gap_9_placement.png`.** **Tests:** whether the CoastSat record puts the groin's effect at the GIS 5/6 boundary, where the model groin sits, or at 6/7. **How to read:** (a) Each point is one CoastSat transect in GIS 4-8, placed by the northing of its landward end from the southern groin; y is its least-squares rate over the annual means of each era (shading ± 1 standard error), seaward positive. Dashed verticals: the two candidate boundaries; red triangles: the groins. (b) The gap across each boundary: the north domain's mean position minus the south domain's, each side about its own 1984-2016 mean, so only its changes read (the size since 1967 is in `../3-photos_vs_coastsat/`). Grey: after the 2017 fill. **Shows:** over 1984-1995, while the groins worked, the rate steps at the southern groin: -7.9 to -4.7 m/yr south of it (GIS 4-5 and transect 0077) against -3.4 to -1.3 m/yr north of it, with no second step at 6/7 (GIS 6 -2.4, GIS 7 -3.3, GIS 8 -2.4 m/yr on average). The gap across 5/6 widened at +4.5 ± 1.1 m/yr over those years and across 6/7 at -0.2 ± 0.6 m/yr. In each later era the step at the southern groin reverses, GIS 4-5 building out or retreating more slowly than the field (the 5/6 gap narrowing), and 6/7 still shows no step. CoastSat starts in 1984, after the fillet had built (photos, `../1-aerial_photo_gap/` figure 6), so these are the rates of a working groin, not the build-up.

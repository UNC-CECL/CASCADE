# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`photos_vs_coastsat_1_what_the_gap_is.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this figure explains the quantity every groin figure and fit uses, and how the photos and CoastSat each measure it. **How to read:** (a) Plan view at true scale, north up, ocean to the right, simplified: GIS 5 and GIS 6 drawn as straight 500 m domains with the groin field at the face between them (in reality the field lies just inside GIS 6), the 1967 shoreline dashed, and the 1996 shoreline at each domain's photo-measured position: GIS 6 -49 m and GIS 5 -183 m from 1967. The gap (purple) is the difference between the two sides' positions. Teal: what the photos measure, the distance from the wet/dry line to a fixed offshore datum line; thin lines: the CoastSat transects whose positions are averaged per side (spacing schematic). (b) Each side's position from the dated aerial-photo wet/dry surveys, change since 1967, seaward positive (`../1-aerial_photo_gap/tables/photo_gap_annual.csv`; two 1972 surveys averaged); the shaded band between them is the gap. (c) How the photo and CoastSat records each measure it, and how to read a change in it. **Shows:** the gap is not new beach: both sides retreated, the north side by 49 m and the south side by 183 m by 1996, and the 135 m between them is the groin's effect.

**`photos_vs_coastsat_2_one_scale.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this shows how the two records are put on one scale before they are compared. **How to read:** squares: each side's position in the dated aerial-photo surveys, change since the 1967 survey (`../1-aerial_photo_gap/`); lines: each side's CoastSat annual mean (`../2-coastsat_gap/` figure 3). (a) As each record comes: CoastSat is centred on its own 1984-2016 mean, so its zero has nothing to do with 1967 and the lines sit far from the squares. (b) Each CoastSat side moved by one constant, the mean difference from the same side's photos over the 13 photo years in 1984-2016 (labelled at each line's start: north -45 m, south -162 m). The shift changes only the level, never the shape, and the gap between the two shifted sides is the CoastSat gap on the photos' scale. **Shows:** CoastSat starts in 1984, 15 years into the groin's life, so its level has to come from the photos; once shifted, the annual lines run through the photo squares on both sides. How closely is figure 3.

**`photos_vs_coastsat_3_gap_and_agreement.png`.** **Tests:** whether the two records agree, side by side and for the gap, once on one scale. **How to read:** (a) The gap, north minus south, from the aerial photos (squares) and from CoastSat annual means (line), both on the photos' 1967 datum (figure 2), so its size since 1967 reads directly. Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events; orange: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. (b) Each photo minus the CoastSat annual mean on that datum, for each side (circles) and the gap (squares). Grey band: ± 2 times the typical scatter of one CoastSat image day about its year's mean (11 m, the median over every year of the side means' per-date standard deviation), the difference a one-day photo would show against an annual mean if the two records agreed exactly. Inside the band is agreement; points outside are outlined. **Shows:** before the fill the two records agree: 13 of 13 north, 13 of 13 south and 12 of 13 gap differences sit inside the band, with RMS differences of 10, 10 and 14 m (the shift makes the mean zero by construction, so only the scatter tests agreement). The one exception is the 2004 gap, +42 m (north +22, south -19), though 2004, after Isabel, is CoastSat's most scattered year, and against that year's own scatter it is 1.8 SD. After the fill both sides of the photos sit landward of CoastSat (north -18 m, south -24 m on average; 2 north and 3 south outside the band) while every gap difference stays inside (+6 m on average): a shift common to both sides, not a disagreement about the groin; a wet/dry line and a satellite waterline could part that way on a wider, re-graded fill beach, which this figure cannot confirm. Per-year spreads are in tables/coastsat_one_day_spread.csv. The annual record fills in what the photos skip: the gap was 142 m in 1996 by CoastSat (135 m in the photos), slipped slowly with no step at Isabel, where the single 2004 photo sits well above CoastSat, and fell to 64 m by 2026 after the fill. The shared shift moves the CoastSat north side by -0 m and the south side by +0 m.

**`photos_vs_coastsat_4_era_rates.png`.** **Tests:** whether the gap widened (groin trapping), held, or narrowed in each era of the structure's history, and whether CoastSat and the aerial photos agree. **How to read:** the least-squares rate of the gap in each era, ± 1 standard error; above zero the north side is gaining on the south. Eras: up to the 1995 last repair, from 1996 to Hurricane Isabel (2003), and from 2004 to the 2017 fill. CoastSat on annual means; photos on the dated wet/dry surveys inside each era (only 1996 and 1997 fall in 1996-2003, too few for a rate); CoastSat on the whole domains, the quantity the model is fitted on. **Shows:** both sources show the gap widening before the repair. From 1996 to 2003 CoastSat shows a slight narrowing that is not distinguishable from zero. After 2004 they disagree: CoastSat is flat while the photos fall, a fall that starts from the single high 2004 survey (figure 3).

**`photos_vs_coastsat_5_state_in_1996.png`.** **Tests:** what state the Buxton groin was in at 1996, the start of the calibration (hindcast) period, from the observations alone. **How to read:** (a) The gap across the groins (GIS 6 seaward of GIS 5) over the groin's whole life: the dated aerial-photo wet/dry surveys as change since 1967 (squares), and the CoastSat annual gap (circles) moved onto the same datum by its mean offset from the photos over the shared 1984-2016 years (117 m), so the two read on one scale. Black line: 1996. Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events (`../../data/structure_history.md`); orange shading: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. (b) The least-squares rate of gap change in each stage, ± 1 standard error: the photos for 1967-1978 (CoastSat starts in 1984), CoastSat for the rest (tables/era_rates.csv). (c) The state in 1996 in words, from (a), (b), `../2-coastsat_gap/` figures 4-9 and the structure record. **Shows:** in 1996 the gap stood at 135 m (photos), 87% of its 155 m peak in 1995, a year after the last repair. It had built at +11.2 m/yr in the first decade and was still widening at +4.5 m/yr to 1995, but from 1996 to 2003 it changed by -2.2 ± 1.8 m/yr, not distinguishable from zero. So in 1996 the groin held a full-sized gap but no longer widened it, and nothing in the record or the data marks a collapse before the 2015-2021 losses.

**`photos_vs_coastsat_6_placement.png`.** **Tests:** what the observed gap is across each candidate boundary for the model groin, GIS 5/6 (where it sits now) or GIS 6/7, so a run with the groin at either can be scored on its own boundary. **How to read:** each panel is the north domain's position minus the south domain's, since 1967, seaward positive: squares from the aerial photos (`../1-aerial_photo_gap/` figure 6), the line from CoastSat (`../2-coastsat_gap/` figure 10) moved onto the photos by the mean difference over their shared 1984-2016 years (+117 m at 5/6, +24 m at 6/7; RMS difference after the shift 14 and 5 m). Bold numbers: the CoastSat gap at 1996, 2009 and 2025, the calibration and test keys (red and blue shading); `tables/placement_targets.csv` holds them with the era rates. **Shows:** the groin's effect is across 5/6. The 5/6 gap built to 155 m (1995) and was 142 m in 1996, 123 m in 2009 and 63 m in 2025. The 6/7 gap peaked at 44 m (1993) and was 31, 15 and 13 m: it did not widen while the groins worked (-0.2 ± 0.6 m/yr over 1984-1995, against +4.5 ± 1.1 at 5/6), and over the calibration it fell 16 m against 19 m at 5/6; over the test it fell 2 m against 60 m. Scored on its own boundary, a groin at 6/7 has a small target that never widened while the groins worked; scored on 5/6, it has to reproduce the larger gap across a boundary it no longer sits on.

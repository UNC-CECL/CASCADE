Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`wave_driven_groin_real_planform.png`.** **Tests:** whether the wave-driven groin (the corrected trapping groin, straight-coast module 6: blocks a fraction of the wave-driven drift arriving at the groin, and gives back near the groin the sand BRIE's row-scaled solve creates or destroys) behaves on the real Buxton shoreline as it did on straight coasts, against the pinned groin the hindcast uses and the drift groin without the correction. **How to read:** BRIE's alongshore solve alone, under option A waves, with no Barrier3D, storms, sea level or source/sink, started from the BRIE shoreline after the first year of the adopted 1996-2025 edgeBE no-groin run and run 30 years. One groin between GIS 5 and GIS 6 (red), with f$_{block}$ = 0.6 throughout: no failure schedule and no fit to the observed fillet. Every value is the groin run minus a no-groin run from the same start. At the start the coast at the groin faces -26 degrees and the updrift approach -10 degrees in BRIE's frame, and the drift runs south, so GIS 6 is updrift. (a) Shoreline change against no groin along GIS 1-20 (GIS 1 at Cape Point) in years 10 (dashed) and 30 (solid). (b) The step across the groin: GIS 6 minus GIS 5, seaward positive. (c) Sand gained across the whole island, summed shoreline metres; zero means the groin neither creates nor destroys sand. Dotted: the correction run with no groin, which shows what it does on its own to the shoreline's natural bends inside its window; the wave-driven curve minus the dotted one is the groin's own sand balance. (d) How much shoreline each module moves per year at the groin, and (dotted) the leak the wave-driven groin returns within 5 cells of the groin. **Shows:** after 30 years the wave-driven groin holds GIS 6 +93 m and GIS 5 -119 m against no groin and changes the island's sand by -50 m, of which -61 m is the correction acting on the natural bend at the groin and +11 m is the groin's own; the drift groin without the correction holds GIS 6 +225 m but changes the island's sand by +173 m; the pinned groin holds GIS 6 +48 m with -90 m. The wave-driven groin moves 56 m in the first year and returns 141 m of leak; by year 30, 55 m and 61 m. The leak it returns is larger than the drift it blocks because Buxton's shoreline already bends at the groin, so the correction also changes BRIE's no-groin behaviour there, by up to 46 m of shoreline. As volume (one 500 m cell, 19.8 m active depth), the uncorrected drift groin's +173 m is +1.7 M m$^3$ over 30 years, and the pinned groin's -0.9 M m$^3$.

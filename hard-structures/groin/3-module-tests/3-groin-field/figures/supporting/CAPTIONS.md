# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`groin_field_profiles.png`.** **Tests:** where each version of the groin module puts the sand when 4 groins share one straight coast, and whether the result looks like a groin field: a sawtooth, each groin holding sand on its updrift side, and the coast cut back just below the last groin. **How to read:** BRIE's alongshore solve alone (option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D, no sea-level rise), 161 domains, 50 years. Rows: the groins 2, 5, 10 and 20 domains apart (1 to 10 km), each row zoomed to its field; columns: three shoreline orientations. Lines are the shoreline change from the straight start, seaward positive, with the updrift side on the right (the net drift runs right to left at every orientation shown); red lines are the groins. Dipole M 12 m/yr; trapping versions blocking fraction 0.6. **Shows:** at -20 degrees every version builds a sawtooth; at 0 degrees the pinned version does nothing, since it blocks only the diffusive part of the transport and the diffusivity is zero there; at +20 degrees the pinned version builds out below each groin instead of above it, and the drift version's field loses 17932 m of summed shoreline at the widest spacing, against +51 m for the corrected version. Each panel has its own vertical range (tables/groin_field_profiles.csv).

**`groin_field_scorecard.png`.** **Tests:** which version of the groin module behaves like a field of 4 groins, on four tests, across 7 shoreline orientations (-30 to +30 degrees, where the drift is at least a tenth of its grid-aligned value) and four spacings. **How to read:** one row per version, one column per test; inside each block, columns are orientations and rows are spacings in domains; a filled square passes. The count is passes out of cases, after 50 years. Conserves sand: the net change across the coast is at most 10% of the gross change. Holds sand at every groin: the cell just updrift of each groin is more than 1 m seaward of the cell just downdrift. Cuts the coast below the field: some cell among the 5 just downdrift of the last groin has retreated more than 1 m. Close groins act as one: each downdrift groin holds less, relative to a single isolated groin, when the groins are 2 domains apart than when they are 20 apart, and at 20 apart holds at least 50% of what the isolated groin holds; it is one result per orientation, repeated down the block. **Shows:** the corrected version passes the first three tests in 25, 28 and 28 of 28 cases, more than any other; it fails conservation only at +20 degrees, where so little sand moves that a small imbalance is a large share, and the fourth test only at +30 degrees, where the drift is a tenth of its grid-aligned value. Even so, its groins 2 domains apart each still hold most of what an isolated groin holds: no version has a groin length to cap the offset. The pinned version conserves sand in 24 cases, four of them at 0 degrees where it moves nothing, and holds sand at every groin in only 12. The dipole and drift versions conserve sand in 0 and 0 of 28 cases (tables/groin_field_scorecard.csv).

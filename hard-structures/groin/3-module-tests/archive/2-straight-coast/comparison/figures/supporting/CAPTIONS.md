# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`straight_coast_modules_vs_orientation.png`.** **Tests:** across shoreline orientations from -40 to +40 degrees, whether each version of the groin module holds sand updrift, and whether the sand balance closes, for the module on its own, for BRIE's alongshore solve, and for the two together. **How to read:** 50 years of BRIE's alongshore solve alone (option A waves), one groin, every value read at year 50. (a) What drives the modules: the wave-climate net longshore drift toward the groin (solid) and BRIE's shoreline diffusivity (dashed); where the diffusivity is at or below zero BRIE clips it to zero (shaded), which freezes a straight coast. (b) How far the cell just updrift of the groin has built out. (c) The sand the module itself adds, as shoreline change summed over the reach; zero means the module's own books balance. (d) The sand BRIE's row-scaled solve adds on top, non-zero wherever the groin makes the diffusivity change along the coast. (e) The total, (c) plus (d): zero means the groin, in the model, neither creates nor destroys sand. Dipole M 12 m/yr; trapping versions blocking fraction 0.6. **Shows:** the net drift is 0.91 M m$^3$/yr toward the groin on a grid-aligned coast and reaches zero near +31 degrees, while the diffusivity is about zero there, so the pinned and conserving versions, which block only the diffusive part of the transport, do nothing at 0 degrees. Only the pinned version stays near zero in (e) (largest |total| 30 m against 15268 m conserving, 1481 m dipole, 4485 m drift, 4076 m hybrid and 27 m corrected), because its own imbalance offsets the solve's; the drift version traps the right quantity but sits on a solve that does not conserve sand, and the hybrid, which adds the pinned version's row correction at the groin face to the drift version, does not close the balance either, because the solve loses sand along the whole bent fillet, not only at the groin face. The corrected version gives that sand back within five cells of the groin each year, and stays near zero while trapping on the updrift side.

**`straight_coast_profiles_year50.png`.** **Tests:** side by side, where each version of the groin module puts the sand after 50 years, at six shoreline orientations. **How to read:** BRIE's alongshore solve alone (option A waves), one groin; shoreline change from the straight start, seaward positive, with the updrift side on the right; the arrow is the net longshore drift toward the groin. A groin that traps drift builds out on the right and cuts back on the left. Each panel has its own vertical range. **Shows:** on coasts turned against the drift (negative angles) every trapping version builds out updrift; at 0 degrees only the drift version and the dipole change the coast; on coasts turned with the drift (positive angles) the pinned version builds out on the wrong (downdrift) side, and the conserving and drift versions cut back both sides, the sand lost through BRIE's solve (summary table: comparison/tables/straight_coast_summary_year50.csv; e.g. at +20 degrees the conserving version's updrift cell is at -1858 m).

**`straight_coast_module_rules.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains the seven groin modules compared in the straight-coast test, by what each one reads and how much sand it moves. **How to read:** (a-g) Plan-view sketches, not to scale: land (sand) above, ocean (blue) below, the updrift side on the right and the net drift arriving from the right. The red line is the groin, between two 500 m cells; the dashed line is the straight coast before the module acts. Each year the module moves the updrift cell's shoreline seaward (shaded) and the downdrift cell's landward (pale) by the amounts marked. (a) The source/sink dipole moves a fixed M = 12 m per year whatever the coast and waves do. (b) The pinned trapping version reads BRIE's own alongshore exchange across the groin, the shoreline step s between the two cells times the diffusivity r, and cancels a fraction f$_{block}$ of it (the blocking fraction); each cell uses its own r, so once the coast bends at the groin the two sides move by different amounts. (c) The conserving version is the same with the groin face's r on both sides, so the two amounts always match. (d) The drift version cancels a fraction f$_{block}$ of the wave climate's net longshore drift Q$_{net}$ arriving at the groin, converted to shoreline metres over one 500 m cell and the 19.8 m active depth; f$_{block}$ = 0.6 in (b)-(g). (e) The hybrid is the drift version plus the pinned version's row correction: the updrift cell also gets f$_{block}$ times the difference between the two cells' r, times the step s, which is what the pinned version moves beyond the conserving one. (f) The corrected version is the drift version plus a leak correction within five cells of the groin: each year it gives back what BRIE's row-scaled solve creates or destroys there, so sand is conserved without changing BRIE. Its first-year transfer is the drift version's. (g) The corrected version with a groin length L (110 m): the same, with f$_{block}$ scaled by (1 $-$ a/L)$^{2/3}$, a the updrift cell's build-out, so it passes all the drift once the fillet reaches the groin's tip; on a straight start a = 0, so its first-year transfer is the corrected version's. (h-n) What each module moves in its first year on a straight coast, against the coast's orientation (its own colour; the others in grey). Below zero (shaded) the module builds the downdrift side instead. Orientations stop at +30 degrees: the net drift reaches zero at +31 degrees and reverses beyond it, which swaps the two sides. **Shows:** the versions differ in what drives them. The dipole moves the same 12 m at every orientation. The pinned and conserving versions read the step across the groin, which on a straight coast is the coast's own tilt, so they move nothing at 0 degrees (no step, and BRIE's diffusivity is zero there), build the updrift side on coasts turned against the drift (+24 m at -30 degrees) and the downdrift side on coasts turned with it (-47 m at +20 degrees). The two are identical in year 1 because a straight coast has one r everywhere; they part once a fillet bends the coast (pinned at +20 degrees, year 10: r$_{up}$ 0.35 vs r$_{down}$ 0.18). Only the drift version follows the drift itself: +55 m at 0 degrees, falling to zero near +31 degrees where the drift stops.

**`straight_coast_trapping_mechanics.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains the mechanics behind the trapping groin modules, why the pinned and conserving versions trap on the wrong side on some coasts, and why only the pinned version conserves sand. **How to read:** (a) On a straight coast at each orientation, the net longshore drift the option A wave climate carries toward the groin (solid; BRIE's own _coast_qs convolved with the wave-angle pdf) and the exchange BRIE's diffusion passes across a cell face, D($\theta$) tan($\theta$) times the 19.8 m active depth (dashed). BRIE moves the shoreline by diffusion only, with D = -(1/depth) dQ/d$\theta$, so a drift that is the same in every cell, the 0.91 M m$^3$/yr at 0 degrees (dotted), moves nothing and never appears; the exchange is zero at 0 degrees, follows the drift's direction on coasts turned against it and runs against it on coasts turned with it (shaded). The drift reaches zero at +32 degrees. (b) At three orientations: the drift, BRIE's exchange, what the pinned and conserving modules block (0.6 of the exchange) and what the drift module blocks (0.6 of the drift); positive is toward the groin, so a positive block builds the updrift side. (c) The bookkeeping at the groin on a bent coast: the pinned run at -30 degrees in year 10, when the fillet has steepened the step across the groin. BRIE's solve is row-scaled: each cell's row uses that cell's own diffusivity r for both its neighbours, so the exchange is counted once with the downdrift r and once with the updrift r, and the two counts differ. The pinned module cancels a share of each count as BRIE makes it; the conserving module uses the groin face's r for both sides. (d) The 50-year sand totals of the two at -30 degrees: what the module adds, what BRIE's solve adds, and the sum. **Shows:** the pinned and conserving modules block a share of BRIE's diffusive exchange, not of the drift: +0.24 M m$^3$/yr at -30 degrees where the drift is 1.06, nothing at 0 degrees where it is 0.91, and -0.47 at +20 degrees, where blocking an exchange that runs against the drift builds the downdrift side. The drift module blocks 0.64, 0.55 and 0.33. Once the coast bends the two sides' r differ (0.096 vs 0.057), so the pinned module takes 56 m from the downdrift cell and gives 33 m to the updrift cell; that mismatch mirrors BRIE's own, and over 50 years the solve hands it back (-1248 m and +1217 m, total -30 m). The conserving module balances its own books, but BRIE's mismatch is then left in place and the reach gains +2327 m.

# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`brie_sand_balance_at_a_step.png`.** **Tests:** whether BRIE's alongshore shoreline step keeps the island's total sand constant when nothing is added or removed. BRIE's step (brie.py:1290-1328) is copied line for line and run on a periodic coast of 30 500 m cells, with the rate table of a real Brie instance (Hs 2 m, Tp 7.5 s, asymmetry 0.6, high-angle fraction 0.5). It is compared with the same step given one rate per boundary, used by both cells it joins. BRIE is unchanged. **How to read:** (a) the step coast at the start and after 50 years. (b) For each boundary near the step, the rate the cell to its south uses for it and the rate the cell to its north uses; BRIE gives each cell the rate of its northern boundary and uses it for both its boundaries, so the two differ wherever the angle changes from one boundary to the next. (c) The change in summed shoreline (seaward +), which is the change in total sand: zero if sand is conserved. **Shows:** the straight coast conserves sand exactly (+0.00 m after 50 years) and the gentle bump nearly (-0.62 m; its steepest angle is 2.4 degrees). At the 100 m step the two sides of a boundary use rates up to 5.69 per 1,000 apart. With these waves BRIE's rate on a coast facing straight into the wave climate is zero (its diffusivity there is negative and clipped), so at each boundary beside the step one side moves and the other does not. The total changes by -23.65 m in 50 years with nothing added or removed. With one rate per boundary it changes by -0.00 m. The two shorelines in (a) differ by up to 25 m at single cells.

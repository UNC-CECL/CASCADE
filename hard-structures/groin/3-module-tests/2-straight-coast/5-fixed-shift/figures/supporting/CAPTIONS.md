# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`straight_coast_fixed_shift.png`.** **Tests:** what the fixed-shift groin (the first version, the source/sink dipole) does on coasts with nothing else going on. It moves a fixed M = 12 m from the downdrift cell to the updrift cell each year, whatever the waves do. **How to read:** (a) The rule each year, in plan view (land above, ocean below, updrift on the right, red the groin). (b, c) The shoreline around the groin in years 1, 5, 10, 25 and 50 (light to dark), on a coast turned against the drift (-20 degrees) and one turned with it (+10 degrees), with the total sand gained or lost across the 61-cell reach by year 50. (d) The updrift (solid) and downdrift (dashed) cells at year 50 against the coast's orientation. (e) The total change across the reach at year 50, in metres of shoreline summed over cells; grey band ±30 m, the pinned groin's range. Orientations stop at +30 degrees, before the drift reverses. BRIE's alongshore step alone under option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D. The two versions side by side are in `straight_coast_fixed_shift_vs_wave_driven.png`. **Shows:** what it builds depends on BRIE, not the drift: at -20 degrees BRIE's solve adds sand and both sides end seaward (updrift +207 m, downdrift +69 m); at +10 degrees it removes sand and both sides end landward (-120 and -310 m), and the reach gains or loses up to 1,481 m.

**`straight_coast_fixed_shift_map.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; the fixed-shift groin's straight-coast runs drawn as maps, so the coast's shape can be seen. **How to read:** plan view at true scale (1:1), north up, ocean to the east, 1.5 km of coast either side of the groin; left the coast at −20 degrees (trends east going north, close to Buxton's own coast at GIS 5-6), right +10 degrees (trends west going north). Dashed: the straight start coast. Grey: the shoreline after 10 years; black: after 50. Gold: sand built out between the start and year 50; red: sand cut back. Red bar: the groin, drawn shore-normal and 250 m long for visibility; in the model it is a face between two 500 m cells, and each cell's shoreline moves as one, which is why the shorelines step. The net longshore drift runs south, so the north side is updrift. Top left of each map: the cells either side of the groin at year 50. Below the key: the wave climate, the same on both coasts. BRIE's alongshore step alone; no storms, no Barrier3D, no sea-level rise. The two versions side by side are in `straight_coast_fixed_shift_vs_wave_driven_map.png`. **Shows:** not the shape of a groin: on the coast turned against the drift both sides build out (+207 / +69 m) and the reach gains +539 m of sand from nowhere; on the coast turned with the drift both sides retreat (-120 / -310 m) and 1,481 m is lost. That is BRIE's solve leaking at the bend the groin makes (`brie_sand_balance_at_a_step.png`), not the groin.

# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`straight_coast_fixed_shift_vs_wave_driven.png`.** **Tests:** how the fixed-shift groin (the first version, the source/sink dipole) and the current candidate (the wave-driven groin, the corrected drift groin) differ on coasts with nothing else going on. **How to read:** left column module 1, right column module 6; the same axes on each row. (a, f) The rule each year, in plan view (land above, ocean below, updrift on the right, red the groin): the fixed-shift groin moves a fixed M = 12 m from the downdrift cell to the updrift cell whatever the waves do; the wave-driven groin holds back a fraction f$_{block}$ = 0.6 of the wave climate's net drift arriving at the groin, and gives back what BRIE's row-scaled solve creates or destroys within 5 cells of it. (b, c, g, h) The shoreline around the groin in years 1, 5, 10, 25 and 50 (light to dark), on a coast turned against the drift (-20 degrees) and one turned with it (+10 degrees), with the total sand gained or lost across the 61-cell reach by year 50. (d, i) The updrift (solid) and downdrift (dashed) cells at year 50 against the coast's orientation. (e, j) The total change across the reach at year 50, in metres of shoreline summed over cells; grey band ±30 m, the pinned groin's range. Orientations stop at +30 degrees, before the drift reverses. BRIE's alongshore step alone under option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D. **Shows:** the fixed-shift groin does not read the waves, so what it builds depends on BRIE, not the drift: at -20 degrees BRIE's solve adds sand and both sides end seaward (updrift +207 m, downdrift +69 m); at +10 degrees it removes sand and both sides end landward (-120 and -310 m), and the reach gains or loses up to 1,481 m. The wave-driven groin builds the updrift side and cuts the downdrift side by the same amount at every orientation (+107 / -107 m at -20 degrees, +164 / -164 m at 0), fading to nothing as the drift falls toward zero, and keeps the reach's sand within 27 m.

**`straight_coast_fixed_shift_vs_wave_driven_map.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this draws the comparison figure's runs as maps, so the coast's shape can be seen. **How to read:** plan view at true scale (1:1), north up, ocean to the east, 1.5 km of coast either side of the groin. Each coast starts straight (dashed) at its orientation: −20 degrees trends east going north, close to Buxton's own coast at GIS 5-6; +10 degrees trends west going north. Grey: the shoreline after 10 years; black: after 50. Gold: sand built out between the start and year 50; red: sand cut back. Red bar: the groin, drawn shore-normal and 250 m long for visibility; in the model it is a face between two 500 m cells, and each cell's shoreline moves as one, which is why the shorelines step. The net longshore drift runs south, so the north side is updrift. Top left of each map: the cells either side of the groin at year 50; bottom: the sand gained or lost over the 61-cell reach, green when within ±30 m. BRIE's alongshore step alone under option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D, no sea-level rise. **Shows:** the wave-driven groin builds a fillet against its north face and cuts the south side back by the same amount on both coasts (+107 / -107 m at −20 degrees, +144 / -144 m at +10), the shape of a real groin, with the sand balanced. The fixed-shift groin does not: on the coast turned against the drift both sides build out (+207 / +69 m) and the reach gains +539 m of sand from nowhere; on the coast turned with the drift both sides retreat (-120 / -310 m) and 1,481 m is lost. That is BRIE's solve leaking at the bend the fixed-shift groin makes (`brie_sand_balance_at_a_step.png`), not the groin.

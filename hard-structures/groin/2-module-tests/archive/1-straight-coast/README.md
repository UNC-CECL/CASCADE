# 2026-10-08-straight-coast

**Question.** Before fitting any groin to Buxton, does each version of the groin module behave like a groin on a coast with nothing else going on? It is tested on straight coasts at orientations from −40° to +40°.

**Setup.** `straight_coast_test.py` is BRIE's alongshore step alone: its own `coast_diff` table, sparse indices and clip at zero, under the option-A waves (Hs 2.0, Tp 7.5, asymmetry 0.6, high-angle 0.5). No storms, no Barrier3D, no sea level. The coast is a straight line at angle θ0 plus a periodic perturbation, so the tilt never meets BRIE's periodic wrap. There are 61 domains and one groin at the middle face, run for 50 years. One step of the emulator matches a real `Brie.update()` exactly (max difference 0.0 m on an arbitrary shoreline).

**The drift.** BRIE moves the shoreline with a diffusivity D(θ). It never uses the net drift itself, which it computes only for inlets (`_coast_qs`). Q_net here is the wave pdf convolved with `_coast_qs`, aligned as BRIE aligns `coast_diff`. That reproduces BRIE's diffusivity: D = −(1/depth) dQ_net/dθ, to within one 1° bin (depth = h_b_crit + d_sf = 19.8 m). On a coast aligned with the grid, 0.91 M m³/yr moves toward lower domain numbers (southward at Hatteras). The drift falls to zero at about +32° and reverses beyond it. Module 4 reads Q_net at the angle of the updrift approach face, the Pelnard-Considère boundary condition. A first version read it across the groin's own step, which rotates the wrong way as the fillet grows; that was corrected.

## Findings

1. **High-angle waves freeze a grid-aligned coast in BRIE.** At 0°, D is −119 m²/yr: high-angle waves (fraction 0.5) cancel the low-angle smoothing. BRIE clips it to 0, which also discards the instability high-angle waves would drive. The drift is still 0.91 M m³/yr. The pinned and conserving groins block only the diffusive part of the transport, so at 0° they do exactly nothing. The drift groin traps there. The dipole adds sand regardless.
2. **The pinned and conserving groins trap on the side set by the coast's tilt, not by the drift.** They block BRIE's diffusive exchange across the groin face, D(θ)·tan θ·depth. That exchange has the sign of Q_net(θ) − Q_net(0), zero at 0°, but is 2-3 times its size (0.40 against 0.15 M m³/yr at −30°): BRIE never moves the part of the drift that is the same in every cell. For θ0 > 0 it runs against the real drift, so they build the "fillet" on the downdrift side (panels d-f of `comparison/figures/straight_coast_profiles_year50.png`). For θ0 < 0 it has the right sign, but it is a small fraction of the drift.
3. **BRIE's alongshore solve does not conserve sand, and a groin is exactly what exposes it.** The row-scaled scheme takes each cell's diffusivity from its own forward face. Where neighbouring faces differ by many degrees, as they do at a groin, it creates sand (θ0 < 0) or destroys it (θ0 > 0). After 50 years this amounts to thousands of metres of summed shoreline for the dipole, conserving and drift modules. On a coast at +10° to +30°, the solve erodes away everything the groin traps.
4. **The pinned groin is the only one that conserves sand at system level.** Its own imbalance offsets the solve's: at every orientation, the total change across the reach stays within 30 m of summed shoreline in 50 years. It cancels a share of BRIE's own row-scaled exchange, so it inherits BRIE's asymmetry with the opposite sign. The "conserving" variant balances the module on paper, but it leaves the solve's error in place. The same holds in the Buxton runs, 1996-2009, all 120 domains, total change against no groin: pinned −22 m, conserving +186 m, dipole +138 m. So the 2026-10-08 finding that the pinned groin "loses 186 m" measured only the module's own books, and the conserving-groin grid was stopped before it ran.
5. **Pelnard-Considère does not apply here.** The drift is so large relative to D·depth that tan α is above 0.2 at every orientation below +25°, so linear theory is invalid. Where it is valid (+25° to +40°, near zero drift), the trapped sand is too small to compare against.
6. **The hybrid does not close the balance (added 2026-10-08).** Adding the pinned module's row correction to the drift groin conserves sand only on coasts turned against the drift, and there it gives back most of the trapping: at −30° the total is −54 m (drift alone +3,084 m), but the updrift cell builds out only 124 m (drift alone 815 m). At 0° to +20° it leaks as much as the drift groin does (−575 m at 0°, −4,076 m at +20°). The correction acts at the groin face only, and the solve's error is spread along the whole bent fillet.
7. **The leak is BRIE's row-scaled solve, not the drift groin.** `straight_coast_conservative_solve_check.py` reruns the drift groin with a face-centred solve, where each face's r is used by both cells it joins, so the sand that leaves one cell arrives in the next. Every orientation then conserves to 0.0 m, and the drift groin builds out the updrift side at every orientation up to the zero-drift angle: 109 m at −30°, 166 m at 0°, 22 m at +20° (`comparison/tables/conservative_solve_check.csv`). With BRIE's own solve the same module gives 815, 67 and −352 m. Most of what the drift groin "traps" at negative angles in BRIE is sand the solve creates.
8. **A callback can return the leak without changing BRIE (module 6, 2026-10-08).** The drift groin plus the per-cell correction within 5 cells of the groin stays within 27 m of zero sand change at every orientation (pinned: 30 m) and builds the updrift side at every orientation up to the drift reversal: 108 m at −30°, 164 m at 0°, 23 m at +20°, matching the face-centred solve. On the real Buxton shoreline the correction also acts on the natural bend at the groin, so it changes BRIE's no-groin behaviour there (`../3-real-planform/corrected_groin/`).
9. **A groin length caps the fillet, but low (module 7, 2026-10-09).** With L = 110 m the updrift cell levels off at 58 m by year 5 and 63 m by year 50 at −20°, 62 m at −30° and 91 m at 0°, and sand is conserved within 10 m. Against a real-sized field on a 25 m grid (`../5-fine-grid-reference/`) that cap is too low and barely grows with L: the rule reads the whole 500 m cell's build-out, which BRIE's diffusion keeps well short of the groin's tip.

## What this means for the module

None of the first five is right. The pinned groin conserves sand but traps the wrong quantity. The drift groin traps the right quantity but sits on a solve that does not conserve sand. A groin that both blocks the real drift and conserves sand would need the shoreline step in flux form, (Q_net(θ_i) − Q_net(θ_{i−1})) / (dy · depth), or the drift groin's injection corrected for the solve's error at its face. The hybrid (module 5) tried the second at the groin face alone, and it is not enough. The conservative-solve check shows the first works: with a face-centred solve the drift groin traps on the correct side and conserves sand exactly. Either is a change to BRIE or to how CASCADE drives it, so it is a decision for later, not part of this test.

**How the corrected groin works, as a GIF (2026-10-09).** `corrected_groin_gif.py` animates module 6 alone at −20° for 60 years: `6-trapping-corrected/figures/corrected_groin_how_it_works.gif` (git-ignored; rerun the script). Each year the groin holds back 60% of the drift arriving, moving it from the downdrift cell to the updrift cell, and puts back BRIE's leak near the groin. What it holds back hardly changes: 58 m of shoreline in year 1, 57.6 m in year 60. The approach face turns from −20° to about −18°, where the drift is almost the same. The gap levels off because BRIE treats the groin face as open and carries sand back across it, about 15 m a year in year 1 and 38 m by year 60, and spreads the rest along the coast. The gap is 104 m after year 1, 204 m at year 25 and 215 m at year 60.

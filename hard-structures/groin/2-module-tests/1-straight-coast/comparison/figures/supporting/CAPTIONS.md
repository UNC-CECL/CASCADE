# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`straight_coast_v1_vs_v6.png`.** **Tests:** how the first groin version (module 1, the source/sink dipole) and the current candidate (module 6, the corrected drift groin) differ on coasts with nothing else going on. **How to read:** left column module 1, right column module 6; the same axes on each row. (a, f) The rule each year, in plan view (land above, ocean below, updrift on the right, red the groin): the dipole moves a fixed M = 12 m from the downdrift cell to the updrift cell whatever the waves do; the corrected groin holds back a fraction f$_{block}$ = 0.6 of the wave climate's net drift arriving at the groin, and gives back what BRIE's row-scaled solve creates or destroys within 5 cells of it. (b, c, g, h) The shoreline around the groin in years 1, 5, 10, 25 and 50 (light to dark), on a coast turned against the drift (-20 degrees) and one turned with it (+10 degrees), with the total sand gained or lost across the 61-cell reach by year 50. (d, i) The updrift (solid) and downdrift (dashed) cells at year 50 against the coast's orientation. (e, j) The total change across the reach at year 50, in metres of shoreline summed over cells; grey band ±30 m, the pinned groin's range. Orientations stop at +30 degrees, before the drift reverses. BRIE's alongshore step alone under option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D. **Shows:** the dipole does not read the waves, so what it builds depends on BRIE, not the drift: at -20 degrees BRIE's solve adds sand and both sides end seaward (updrift +207 m, downdrift +69 m); at +10 degrees it removes sand and both sides end landward (-120 and -310 m), and the reach gains or loses up to 1,481 m. The corrected groin builds the updrift side and cuts the downdrift side by the same amount at every orientation (+107 / -107 m at -20 degrees, +164 / -164 m at 0), fading to nothing as the drift falls toward zero, and keeps the reach's sand within 27 m.

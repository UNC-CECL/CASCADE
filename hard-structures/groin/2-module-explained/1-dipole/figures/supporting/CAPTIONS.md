# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`dipole_1_in_the_model_year.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this figure explains where the source/sink dipole groin (straight-coast version 1, the runner's groin until 2026-10-13) acted in a CASCADE model year. **How to read:** (a) the order within one year: every Barrier3D domain runs first, including its background erosion (BE), and hands BRIE its shoreline change for the year; the dipole then edits that change in the two domains either side of the groin, adding M seaward to GIS 6 (north, updrift) and M landward to GIS 5 (south, downdrift); BRIE then moves the shoreline alongshore. The dipole was a callback on the sandbox Cascade (`cascade/cascade_groin.py`, `_groin_callback`), not part of the BE rates, though it entered the same number BE ends up in. (b) an illustrative example of the edit: grey, a uniform 1.5 m of retreat from Barrier3D in every domain (made up for the example); dark, what BRIE receives, the same except GIS 5 and GIS 6, moved 12 m apart (gold). **Shows:** the dipole is a fixed transfer between two neighbouring domains each year, set by one number (M) and blind to the waves; everything else that happens to the groin's shoreline is BRIE's doing (figure dipole_2).

**`dipole_2_explained.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains how the source/sink dipole groin (straight-coast version 1) worked and why its sand did not balance. The companion of `corrected_groin_explained.png` (`../2-corrected/figures/`). **How to read:** (a) a plan-view sketch, not to scale: land above, ocean below, the updrift side (GIS 6, north) on the right, the dashed line the coast before the groin. Each year: (1) the dipole moves a fixed M = 12 m of shoreline from the domain south of the groin to the one north of it, whatever the waves are doing; (2) BRIE's alongshore step smooths the result along the coast, not knowing a groin is there; (3) at the bend this makes, BRIE's step makes or loses sand, because each domain takes its alongshore rate from its own boundary (`../../3-module-tests/2-straight-coast/3-sand-balance-at-a-step/figures/brie_sand_balance_at_a_step.png`), and nothing puts it back. (b) and (c), from the straight-coast runs (BRIE's alongshore step alone, option A waves, 50 years): (b) the sand the dipole moves each year (it balances: GIS 6 gains what GIS 5 loses) against the sand BRIE's step makes or loses each year, on a coast turned against the drift and one turned with it; (c) the running total over the 61-domain reach; grey, the ±30 m band the corrected groin stays inside. **Shows:** the dipole's own transfer is balanced, but the bend it builds keeps steepening (the face angle goes from −20 to -33 degrees on the first coast, from +10 to -11 on the second), and BRIE's leak grows with it: by year 50 it makes +18 m a year on the first and -29 m a year on the second, more than the dipole's 12 m, and the reach has gained +539 m and lost 1,481 m. What the dipole built at Buxton therefore depended mostly on the coast's angle at the groin, not on the drift it is meant to trap; that is why it was replaced by the corrected groin.

**`dipole_3_steps.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure shows where the source/sink dipole groin (version 1) acted in a CASCADE model year, and the shape it builds, starting from a straight coast. The dipole sat on the sandbox Cascade's hook (`cascade/cascade_groin.py`, before 563a542e). **How to read:** (a) the order within one call of `Cascade.update()`: Barrier3D in every domain (storms, overwash, dunes, background erosion), then the groin hook (`_groin_callback`), then BRIE's alongshore transport, then the management modules; then the next year. (b)-(e) the first year of the straight-coast run, from a straight coast at -20 degrees (BRIE's alongshore step alone, option A waves): the shoreline at the start, after Barrier3D (unchanged: the test has none; at Buxton storms and background erosion act here), after the groin's edit (arrows), and after BRIE smooths it alongshore (dashed: the shoreline before each step). (f) the same year repeated for 50 years, on its own scale (dashed: the straight start). Red: the groin, the face between GIS 5 and GIS 6. **Shows:** each year the dipole cuts a 12 m notch and adds a 12 m bump either side of the groin, and BRIE spreads them; in year 1 BRIE makes no sand (+0.0 m), but as the step steepens it does, and after 50 years the reach has gained +539 m of sand that was never added (`dipole_2_explained.png`). The companion of `../2-corrected/figures/corrected_groin_steps.png`.

Where does the sand go? Working BRIE's step by hand

You will do, with a pencil and this page, exactly what BRIE does to the shoreline in one year: work out each section's angle, turn it into a rate, update the positions, and then check whether the sand adds up. Every number here is BRIE's own, from an unmodified brie.Brie with only its alongshore step switched on (option A waves: Hs 2.0 m, Tp 7.5 s, asymmetry 0.6, high-angle fraction 0.5).

The coast has six sections, 500 m each, on BRIE's looped coast (the last section's north neighbour is section 0). A 100 m headland covers sections 2, 3 and 4. BRIE stores each section's shoreline position x measured landward, so the headland sections have the smaller x.

STEP 1Read the angle line

This is how BRIE works out each section's shoreline angle (brie.py, lines 820–828, condensed):

theta = 180 * np.arctan2( x_s[np.r_[1:ny, 0]] - x_s , dy ) / np.pi

np.r_[1:ny, 0] builds a list of index numbers: the numbers from 1 up to (but not including) ny, then a 0 on the end. Here ny = 6.

STEP 2Each section's angle

For each section, take its north neighbour's position minus its own, then θ[i] = atan( (x[i+1] − x[i]) / 500 m ) in degrees. (Section 5's north neighbour is section 0.)

Section 1: its north neighbour is section 2, so x[2] − x[1] = 900 − 1000 = −100, and atan(−100 / 500) = atan(−0.2) = −11.3°. Most sections have a flat north side and an angle of 0.

STEP 3Turn each angle into one rate

BRIE looks up the wave climate's diffusivity D at the angle (its table coast_diff, lines 1294–1309), then r[i] = max(0, D × dt / 2 / dy²) = max(0, D / 500,000) with dt = 1 year and dy = 500 m. A negative D becomes a rate of 0.

STEP 4Update the positions

The update (lines 1315–1324) uses the positions on both sides, but only the one rate you found:

change[i] = r[i] × ( x[i+1] − 2·x[i] + x[i−1] )
          = r[i] × ( x[i+1] − x[i] )   +   r[i] × ( x[i−1] − x[i] )
            └─ exchange with north ─┘     └─ exchange with south ─┘

Section 1: with north = 0.018 × (900 − 1000) = −1.84; with south = 0.018 × (1000 − 1000) = 0. Any section with rate 0 has both parts 0, however steep its sides are.

STEP 5Balance the books at each boundary

Every boundary is shared by two sections, and each writes down its own number for the exchange across it: the section to the south from its "with north" column, the section to the north from its "with south" column. If sand only moved across the boundary, the two would cancel. Copy them across from Step 4 and add them up.

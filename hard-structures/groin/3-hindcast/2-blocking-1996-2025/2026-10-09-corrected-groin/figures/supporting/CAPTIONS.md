# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`corrected_groin_1_strength_over_time.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this figure shows the options compared in the corrected-groin hindcast. **How to read:** each line is the corrected groin's strength, the share of the wave-climate net drift arriving at the groin that it holds back each year (0.16 at Buxton is about 15 m of shoreline a year). Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events (`../../../1-observations/data/structure_history.md`); shading: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. Arrows: the calibration (1996-2009) and test (2009-2025) periods. The pinned groin in the runner now is not drawn: its 0.6 is a share of BRIE's diffusive exchange, not of the drift, so it is not on this axis. **Shows:** the two-number groin holds one strength until the 2017 fill and nothing after; its strength is fitted on 1996-2009 and its end year is taken from the record, not fitted.

**`corrected_groin_2_gap_over_time.png`.** **Tests:** whether the corrected groin reproduces the observed gap across the Buxton groins, in the calibration period it was fitted to and in the test period it was not. **How to read:** the change in the gap between GIS 6 (updrift, north) and GIS 5 (downdrift, south) since each period's start, from its own starting topography; positive means the gap widened. Teal: observed, annual CoastSat (open circles, joined) and the wet/dry photo dates (squares, dashed). Lines: no groin (grey) and the two-number corrected groin at 0.16 (solid black, fitted to annual CoastSat) and 0.2 (dashed, fitted to both records), each off from the 2017 fill. **Shows:** without a groin the gap closes far too fast in both periods. Row k is calendar year start + k; each period starts on its start year's calendar-mean line. In the test the corrected groin, off from the fill, follows the observed gap through (-60 m at the end against -60 m observed and -61 m with no groin).

**`corrected_groin_3_choosing_strength.png`.** **Tests:** which strength the two-number corrected groin should have, and which observed record to fit it to. **How to read:** (a) the root-mean-square error in the gap across the groins over the calibration period, 1996-2009, against the groin's strength, scored on the annual CoastSat gap (solid) and on the wet/dry photo dates (dashed); dotted lines mark the strength each target picks and the one that balances them. (b) Each of those strengths, and a weaker one, run on the test period, 2009-2025, with the groin off from the 2017 fill: the annual CoastSat error before (filled circles) and after (open squares) the fill; grey lines are the run without a groin. Only 2010-2016 played no part in any choice. **Shows:** the annual record wants 0.16, the photos 0.25, both together 0.2. On the test the error rises with strength before the fill: 12.0 m at 0.12, 18.5 m at 0.16, 28.4 m at 0.2 and 39.6 m at 0.25, against 32.4 m with no groin. The annual-fitted strength beats no groin; the photo-fitted one does worse. The test would prefer a groin weaker than any calibration target picks.

**`corrected_groin_4_test_summary.png`.** **Tests:** how each version of the corrected groin does on the test period, 2009-2025, which was not used to fit its strength, split at the 2017 Buxton fill. **How to read:** one row per run; the filled circle is the annual CoastSat error in the gap across the groins for 2010-2016, the open square for 2017-2025; grey lines mark the run without a groin. All runs on the calendar-year setup; the pinned and drift groins were only run on the earlier six-month setup and are not shown. **Shows:** every groin that stays on after 2017 misses 2017-2025 by 20-47 m, because it holds the fill's sand at GIS 6 while the observed sand passed the groin field; switching the groin off at the fill brings that half to 8-12 m, still above no groin (5.2 m). Before the fill the two-number groin fitted to annual CoastSat (0.16) scores 18.5 m against 32.4 m with no groin; the weaker 0.12 scores 12.0 and 8.2 m, the best of any run. The 2017 switch-off was suggested by the test misfit and is supported by the placement record, so only 2010-2016 is fully independent (README).

**`corrected_groin_5_plan_view.png`.** **Tests:** what the groin versions look like on a map against the observed shoreline, at the end of the calibration, just before the 2017 fill and at the end of the test. **How to read:** each map is the same 1.5 km window centred on the Buxton groin field (GIS 5-7), drawn at true scale, north up (UTM 18N); white lines and labels are the GIS domain bands. Sand fill and the thick teal line: the observed CoastSat shoreline that year (the median position per transect). Dashed grey: the observed shoreline over the run's start window, where each modelled line begins (1995-1997 for the calibration run, 2008-2010 for the test run). Thin lines: the modelled shorelines, each placed per transect as that start position plus the model's shoreline change in the transect's domain, along the transect's shore-normal. The model has one value per 500 m domain, so its lines step at the domain edges; that is its resolution, not an error in the drawing. The small table on each map is each run's mean offset from the observed shoreline in GIS 5 (south of the groins) and GIS 6 (the groin field), seaward positive. **Shows:** at true scale the runs differ by tens of metres in a 1.5 km window, so the table carries the comparison. In 2008, without a groin GIS 6 sits 39 m landward of the observed shoreline; with the corrected groin 15 m. In 2024 the pinned groin, still on, holds GIS 6 27 m seaward of the observed shoreline and GIS 5 4 m landward of it, so its gap is too wide; the corrected groin, off from the 2017 fill, is +8 and +23 m. The corrected groin's modelled gap change since the 2009 start is -25 m in 2016 and -60 m in 2024, against -8 m and -50 m observed. In 2024 every run sits 40-50 m seaward of the observed shoreline at GIS 7-8, north of the window's groins: the 2017 fill's sand stays where it was placed in the model, a separate misfit the groin does not touch.

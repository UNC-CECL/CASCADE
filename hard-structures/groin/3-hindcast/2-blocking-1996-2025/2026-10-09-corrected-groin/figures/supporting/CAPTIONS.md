# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`corrected_groin_1_strength_over_time.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this figure shows the options compared in the corrected-groin hindcast. **How to read:** each line is the corrected groin's strength, the share of the wave-climate net drift arriving at the groin that it holds back each year (0.12 at Buxton is about 11 m of shoreline a year). Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events (`../../../1-observations/structure_history.md`); shading: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. Arrows: the calibration (1996-2009) and test (2009-2025) periods. The pinned groin in the runner now is not drawn: its 0.6 is a share of BRIE's diffusive exchange, not of the drift, so it is not on this axis. **Shows:** the two-number groin holds one strength until the 2017 fill and nothing after; its strength is fitted on 1996-2009 and its end year is taken from the record, not fitted.

**`corrected_groin_2_gap_over_time.png`.** **Tests:** whether the corrected groin reproduces the observed gap across the Buxton groins, in the calibration period it was fitted to and in the test period it was not. **How to read:** the change in the gap between GIS 6 (updrift, north) and GIS 5 (downdrift, south) since each period's start, from its own starting topography; positive means the gap widened. Teal: observed, annual CoastSat (open circles, joined) and the wet/dry photo dates (squares, dashed). Lines: no groin (grey), the pinned groin in the runner now (purple), and the two-number corrected groin at 0.12 (solid black, fitted to annual CoastSat) and 0.19 (dashed, fitted to both records), each off from the 2017 fill. **Shows:** without a groin the gap closes far too fast in both periods. In the test, the pinned groin follows the observed gap until 2017 and then holds it open while the observed gap closes; the corrected groin, off from the fill, follows it through (-54 m at the end against -50 m observed and -58 m with no groin).

**`corrected_groin_3_choosing_strength.png`.** **Tests:** which strength the two-number corrected groin should have, and which observed record to fit it to. **How to read:** (a) the root-mean-square error in the gap across the groins over the calibration period, 1996-2009, against the groin's strength, scored on the annual CoastSat gap (solid) and on the wet/dry photo dates (dashed); dotted lines mark the strength each target picks and the one that balances them. (b) Each of those three strengths run on the test period, 2009-2025, with the groin off from the 2017 fill: the annual CoastSat error before (filled circles) and after (open squares) the fill; grey lines are the run without a groin. Only 2009-2016 played no part in any choice. **Shows:** the annual record wants 0.12, the photos 0.25. On the test the annual-fitted strength is best before the fill (11.5 m, against 24.6 m for 0.19, 39.5 m for 0.25 and 31.7 m with no groin) and after it (12.0 m). The photo-fitted strength does worse than no groin before the fill. Its pull toward a stronger groin rests on the 2004 survey, where the photos and CoastSat disagree by 42 m.

**`corrected_groin_4_test_summary.png`.** **Tests:** how each version of the groin does on the test period, 2009-2025, which was not used to fit its strength, split at the 2017 Buxton fill. **How to read:** one row per run; the filled circle is the annual CoastSat error in the gap across the groins for 2009-2016, the open square for 2017-2024; grey lines mark the run without a groin. The pinned groin is the one in the runner now (b 0.6, f 0.6); the drift groin is the corrected groin without its leak correction. **Shows:** every groin that stays on after 2017 misses 2017-2024 by 25-52 m, because it holds the fill's sand at GIS 6 while the observed sand passed the groin field; switching the groin off at the fill brings that half to 12-17 m. Before the fill, the pinned groin does best (7.5 m), then the weaker drift and corrected groins (11.5-11.9 m); the stronger corrected groins do worse. The two-number groin fitted to annual CoastSat (0.12, off from 2017) is best on both halves of the corrected runs (11.5 and 12.0 m). The 2017 switch-off was suggested by the 2017-2024 misfit and is supported by the placement record, so only 2009-2016 is fully independent (README).

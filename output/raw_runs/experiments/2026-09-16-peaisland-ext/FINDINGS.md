# 2026-09-16-peaisland-ext: what the runs say

The tables are in `RESULTS.md` (regenerated by `HAT_peaisland_extension.py
score`); the figures are `figures/compare_main_vs_extension_gis1_90.png`
(read this first) and `figures/alongshore_rates_extension.png`. Numbers
below are from the 2026-09-16 evening solves, on the extension numbered by
Hannah's whole-island domain polygons. Interior RMSE is always GIS 2-89
against the surveyed CoastSat target. "Boundary term" is the source/sink
rate imposed at an end domain of the modelled reach (the edgeBE preset), in
m/yr; the matrix calls these the locked end-domain values.

## Compressed planform (the calibration everything else stands on)

| run | interior RMSE | interior bias | GIS 80-90 RMSE | southern boundary term | northern boundary term |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| base, zeroBE (matrix) | 1.0852 | -0.223 | 0.945 | 0 | 0 |
| base, edgeBE (matrix) | 1.1407 | +0.029 | 1.342 | +32.2 at GIS 1 | +10.0 at GIS 90 |
| n115, zeroBE | 1.1121 | -0.220 | 0.937 | 0 | 0 |
| n115, edgeBE solved | 1.0981 | -0.018 | 0.937 | +37.1 at GIS 1 | +42.0 at GIS 115 |

1. **The buffer's orientation, by itself, barely moves the interior.** With
   no boundary terms anywhere (zeroBE), swapping the extrapolated buffer for
   the measured Pea Island coast changes the interior RMSE from 1.085 to
   1.112 and the bias not at all (-0.223 to -0.220). On GIS 80-90 the two
   zeroBE curves differ by at most 0.02 m/yr, at GIS 89-90. Alongshore
   diffusion in the compressed planform does not carry the boundary's shape
   12 km into the reach.

2. **What the extension removes is the footprint of the GIS 90 boundary
   term.** In the base, +10.0 m/yr at GIS 90 lifts GIS 85-90 by up to 1.4
   m/yr (GIS 90: -1.46 zeroBE, -0.14 edgeBE) and that is why the base edgeBE
   run scores WORSE than base zeroBE on the interior (1.141 vs 1.085) and on
   GIS 80-90 (1.342 vs 0.945). With the north end moved to GIS 115 the
   boundary term there does not reach GIS 80-90 at all (difference to the
   zeroBE run 0.002 m/yr), so the solved n115 run keeps zeroBE's near-end
   rates and lands at 1.098 interior RMSE: better than base edgeBE by 0.04,
   with the +0.03 bias replaced by -0.02. That improvement is the artefact
   leaving, not skill arriving.

3. **The northern boundary term does not get smaller; it gets bigger.** GIS
   115 needs +42.0 m/yr where GIS 90 needed +10.0. Its target is +3.4 m/yr
   (CoastSat, one-sided LOESS at the end of the extension, accreting toward
   the inlet) and the unforced model gives -0.6 there. So the end value was
   never correcting the buffer's shape: it supplies an observed accretion
   the model cannot produce, and that is larger at Pea Island's north end
   than at Rodanthe. Moving the boundary moves the problem.

4. **The southern boundary term is a Cape Point fact.** GIS 1 needs +32 to
   +37 m/yr in every compressed run because its raw target is +3.2 m/yr.
   (A one-domain southern extension, run and removed the same evening because
   no domain polygon lies south of GIS 1, had needed only +3.2 at GIS 0 and
   left GIS 1 unpinned, which put the interior bias back at -0.21: the +0.03
   bias of the base edgeBE run is the GIS 1 boundary term spilling accretion
   into GIS 2-10. Whether that spill is wanted is a judgement about Cape
   Point, which the model does not represent.)

5. **The south end feels the north end through the periodic ring.** GIS 1's
   solved value rose from +32.2 (base) to +37.1 (n115) although nothing
   south of it changed: its zeroBE residual went from -2.93 to -3.43 because
   the ring's closing bridge now starts from GIS 115's offset rather than
   GIS 90's. A one-sided extension still changes both ends.

## Detrended planform (full strength, for a bound)

| run | interior RMSE | interior bias | southern boundary term | northern boundary term |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base, zeroBE | 8.98 | -3.82 | 0 | 0 |
| base, edgeBE solved | 9.03 | -3.93 | +90.0 at GIS 1 | -84.4 at GIS 90 |
| n115, zeroBE | 8.95 | -4.43 | 0 | 0 |
| n115, edgeBE solved | 8.61 | -3.76 | +109.4 at GIS 1 | -117.6 at GIS 115 |

6. **At full strength the planform, not the buffer, is the problem.** Every
   detrended run has an interior RMSE near 9 m/yr and a bias near -4, the
   over-response found on 2026-08-21 when the /10 unit error was first
   corrected (RMSE 7.9-8.9). The ends need boundary terms of +90 to +109
   south and -84 to -118 north: the model moves the island's sand north at a
   rate the observations do not show, and no boundary placement changes
   that. Extending the reach changes the interior RMSE by 0.4 out of 9.

## What this settles

The extended geometry is a cleaner boundary treatment for the northern end
in the compressed planform (no boundary-term footprint inside GIS 80-90,
interior RMSE 1.098 vs 1.141) but it does not shrink the end values, and it
does not change any conclusion about the interior. The buffer's orientation
is not a lever. If the north-end value is to mean something physical it
would have to be placed AT Oregon Inlet (GIS 119-120, the last domains with
a dune line) and read as the inlet's sediment budget, which is a different
experiment and a different claim.

## Provenance

Runs under this folder, one member per configuration with its Newton steps
inside it (`<member>/step<k>/`; `SOLVED` names the one that stands); every
run's index row carries `geometry`, its end values and
`rmse_reach_interior_m_yr`. Inputs: `2-brie-offset/1996/ext/n115/`,
`2-brie-offset/raw_offsets/ext/`, `5-scr/coastsat_lrr/1996_2010/ext/`, all
numbered by `1-barrier3d-domains/domain-geojson/domains_pea_hatteras_120.geojson`
through the surveyed reach's own join rules (line intersects polygon for the
dune-line transects, origin point within polygon for CoastSat; both
reproduce the surveyed assignments exactly). An earlier numbering by
northing bin, and a one-domain southern extension, were run first and
deleted the same evening; the northing bin had placed 4 dune-line and 40
CoastSat transects one domain off, which moved the GIS 115 offset by 46 m
and its target by +0.2 m/yr and the solved northern term from +41.7 to
+42.0. Baselines are the current 1996-2010 matrix runs (v2 offsets,
1984-start CURRENT topography). `base-check` reproduced the matrix edgeBE
row exactly through the geometry-aware runner. Every end solved to a
residual of 0.005 m/yr or better, except GIS 1 in base-detrended (-0.023).

# Captions — vs_shoreline_and_duneline

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`model_vs_shoreline_and_duneline_1984_2004.png`.** The two scoring targets and modelled shoreline change rate by GIS domain (1 at Cape Point, 90 at Pea Island) for 1984–2004. The two coloured lines are the two observations, BOTH AS NET CHANGE between the same two dune-line dates over the survey interval, each given the scoring target's treatment (a 10-domain LOESS of the transect rates north of domain 10, and the raw domain means over domains 1–10 where the Oregon Inlet boundary dominates). Blue is the CoastSat shoreline: the mean satellite position within six months of each dune-line date, differenced (5-scr/3-rates/coastsat/endpoint). Red is the digitised dune line, end line minus start line (5-scr/3-rates/duneline/endpoint). Vintages and dates: 1984–2004: the 1984 line (1984-09-19) to the 2004 line (2004-05-25), 19.68 yr. The gap between them is beach-width change, which the model, whose shoreline is a dune line behind a fixed berm, cannot represent. The CoastSat LRR, the model's scoring target, is drawn under vs_shoreline/. The two black lines are the modelled shoreline change of the same window, the endpoint rate, the run's last annual shoreline minus its first over the run years, like both observations here, from the edgeBE source/sink preset under full management with the groin off, each against the target its two end domains were solved on: solid with the two end domains solved against the CoastSat target (1984–2004: HAT_1984_2004_edgeBE_road_bdm_nogroin (version-pair/v2 arm)); dashed with the two end domains solved against the dune-line change (mean3 reading, endpoint estimator; experiments/2026-09-16-dune-edgesolve) instead of CoastSat (1984–2004: HAT_1984_2004_edgeBE_road_bdm_nogroin (2026-09-16-dune-edgesolve/mean3/step2 arm)). A dune line and a shoreline are different features, so a gap between the two curves is beach-width change as much as model misfit. Village spans are shaded; the solid hairline is the Buxton groin and the dotted hairlines are the Avon and Rodanthe piers. The y axis is held at ±10 m/yr on every panel, the largest |rate| over every observed reading and modelled curve of every window plus 1 m rounded up, so the panels are directly comparable.

NCDOT criteria for NC 12 dune management: what the documents say
Compiled 2026-11-11 from NCDOT, FHWA and NC Division of Coastal Management documents.
Page numbers are the document's own unless marked "PDF p."

BOTTOM LINE
- No NCDOT document found sets a distance at which dunes are rebuilt. Any model gate is our
  translation of their criteria.
- The best-supported, citable criterion is the 230 ft (70.1 m) buffer from mean high water to
  the edge of pavement. It was derived from NC 12 maintenance records, and NCDOT ties it to dune
  protection.
- 230 ft (70 m) less the island's ~50 m median beach is ~20 m behind the dune, the hindcast's
  default gate. (The ~50 m median beach is taken from the code comments and has not been
  recomputed.)
- The 150 m gate (198 m less the beach) has no NCDOT basis. Its only support is the lidar,
  with rebuilds seen up to 140 m behind the dune.

CORRECTION NEEDED IN OUR CODE AND FIGURE TEXT
Our text says "NCDOT maintains the dune where NC 12 is within 650 ft (198 m) of the shoreline
(FEIS 2008, p. 2-128)". That is not what p. 2-128 says. There, 650 ft is one of several possible
"warning signs" for a monitoring program that decides when to build bridge Phases III and IV:
  "A distance between the shoreline and the road of less than 650 feet (198 meters) (650 feet is
  based on measurements of the landward extent of washover fans that developed during Hurricane
  Isabel and should generally allow natural shoreline processes to occur without notable effects
  on NC 12 operations)"
It is a distance at which the road is comfortably safe, not a dune maintenance limit.

NCDOT CRITERIA
1. 230 ft (70.1 m) vulnerability buffer, from mean high water to the edge of pavement
   - FEIS p. 2-64: "When the distance from the edge of NC 12's pavement to the active shoreline
     becomes less than or equal to the buffer width of 230 feet (70.1 meters), the vulnerability
     of NC 12 to short-term storm impacts can be mitigated by the presence of dunes."
   - R-3116B (PDF p. 16) and R-4070B (PDF p. 15): "the 230 foot distance was determined through
     an empirical analysis of roadway maintenance data."
   - Monitoring report 2019 (PDF p. 22): it originated in Stone, Overton and Fisher (1991),
     which proposed 230 ft "to indicate when a coastal highway became vulnerable to repetitive
     overwash and sand deposits resulting in excessive maintenance costs", based on "NCDOT
     maintenance records for NC 12."
   - It is applied island-wide: in the Refuge (FEIS), Frisco-Hatteras Village (R-3116B) and
     Avon-Buxton (R-4070B).
2. Dune placement about 25 ft (7.6 m) from the edge of pavement
   - R-3116B (PDF p. 17) and R-4070B (PDF p. 15): "NCDOT recommends placing dunes approximately
     25 feet from the edge of NC 12 pavement." Their design dune is 10 ft high, with a 15 ft
     crest, a 3:1 road-side slope and a 5:1 ocean-side slope.
   - FEIS p. 2-128: "the frequency of dune maintenance is highest when a dune is less than
     25 feet [7.6 meters] from the road."
3. When to replenish a dune
   - FEIS p. 2-64: "Dunes would need to be replenished once 50 percent of their volume was lost."
   - Monitoring report 2019 (PDF p. 2): one vulnerability indicator is a dune crest elevation
     less than 10 ft above the NC 12 centerline.

HOW NCDOT DESCRIBES THE MAINTENANCE (FEIS, Oregon Inlet to Rodanthe)
- p. 4-68: dune maintenance, dune rebuilding and sandbag-based dunes are "generally storm related
  activities." Whether and how often they occur increases with:
  (1) decrease in the distance between NC 12 and the shoreline, (2) degradation of the dunes,
  (3) storm magnitude, (4) storm frequency, (5) sediment supply.
- Table 4-13 (p. 4-69):
  - Road scraping: 1-2 times a month.
  - Minor dune maintenance: 2-3 times a year.
  - Dune rebuilding: 1-2 times a year. Bulldozers push sand into a dune from the landward side,
    with sand from the landward shoulder of the easement or another source on the island, and
    "no beach scraping."
  - Sandbag-core dune with berm: 1-2 times a year.
- p. 2-144: this work is concentrated in the three Refuge hot spots (the 'S' Curves, the Sandbag
  Area and the Canal Zone).
- p. 4-68 and p. xxii: after the Record of Decision, maintenance in the Refuge is confined to the
  100 ft NC 12 easement. Table 4-13 notes that earlier work often went partly or completely
  outside it. This conflicts with the lidar's 140 m rebuilds, at least in the Refuge after 2008.

PERMIT (Coastal Resources Commission variance files CRC-VR-24-12 and 24-13, December 2024)
- CAMA Major Permit 86-99, first issued 4 September 1999 and modified many times since,
  authorizes NCDOT to perform dune maintenance along NC 12 on Hatteras Island. It was last
  renewed in December 2021 for five years.
- "Dune maintenance is authorized in specific areas where DCM has previously reviewed and
  authorized" this work. Those mapped areas are the closest thing to a formal "where" rule. The
  maps are exhibits in the variance file ("Maps from previous 86-99 approval areas").
- Example: after the 15-17 November 2024 storm, NCDOT rebuilt the primary dune at the Pea Island
  Visitor Center under the permit's maintenance clause.
- 15A NCAC 07H .0308: temporary sandbags may protect a road only if it is "imminently
  threatened": its right-of-way is less than 20 ft from the erosion scarp. This is for sandbags,
  not dunes.

# Collision module testing, 2026-11-09 to 11-11

What was tested on the collision module in these three days, why, what it found, and where the figures and runs are.
In order: the Automorph toes, toes per cell against per domain, the Cs sweeps, why the dunes north of Buxton stay too
high, the road manager's first-year bulldozing (found and fixed), the toe at the berm (Laura's suggestion), the two
dune rows, the 2009 lidar datum, and three new module options (row modes, lateral erosion, collapse tracking).

All hindcasts are 1996-2009 (`1-calibration-1996-2009/HAT_dune_collision.py`; runs and logs in the workspace's
`output/raw_runs/dune-storm-response/2026-10-26-dune-collision/`). Unless a section says otherwise they use the
**module-only setup**: dune growth 0.02-0.04, groin 0.16, no road rule, Rodanthe 40%, Larson et al. (2004) alone,
so that the collision module is the only thing that differs from the module-off run. Figures are in the workspace's
`dune-collision/4-hindcast/3-on-vs-off/` topic folders (`1-module-on-off/`, `3-cs-and-toes/`, `6-other-tests/`), with
captions in each folder's `supporting/CAPTIONS.md`. Crest scores are model against lidar domain-median crests per
domain and survey (`dune_crest_vs_lidar.csv` of each run), GIS 6-90 where the module runs, unless stated.

**Design names (from 2026-11-12).** A design is named "erosion, start, Cs"; figures, captions and this log use these
names, and the run folders keep theirs. Earlier sections use the older words in brackets.

| Erosion | What it does |
|---|---|
| No collision (module off) | no storm erosion of the dune; it only grows and is overwashed |
| Lower both rows (proportional) | the eroded volume taken from both rows in proportion to their height |
| Lower front row (front only) | the eroded volume taken from the seaward row only |
| Face retreat (lateral) | the seaward face cut back: the front row keeps its height until its sand is gone, then the back row |

| Start | What it is |
|---|---|
| Equal rows (v3, both rows at max) | both rows at the highest cell of each profile's hand-picked window |
| Low back row (maxmed) | the front row at the window's highest cell, the back row at its median |

## 4. Why GIS 9-13 stay too high (2026-11-09)

**What.** Storm peak water levels against the toe, the measured toe at each survey and the crests
(`3-cs-and-toes/automorph_domain_toes_storm_vs_toe_GIS9-15.png`). **Found.** The measured toe dropped about 1 m by
1998-2001 (GIS 10-12: 3.1 to 2.1 m), but the model holds it at its 1996 value (3.4-3.6 m): before 2003 one storm
reaches the fixed toe, 12-33 the measured one. The lidar crests at GIS 10-12 dropped 1.5-2.5 m in 1998-1999, some
before Dennis (GIS 11: -1.3 m to 1998, peak water 2.64 m), and to about the storm water level (GIS 10: 3.61 m after a
3.65 m Dennis), which collision erosion cannot do; the island-wide water levels (Duck gauge plus Stockdon, slope
0.06) are likely too low at Buxton, and NC-12 was relocated in GIS 9-14 in 1999. The toe stays fixed (Hannah,
2026-11-10): a limitation, not tuned around.

**What happened to those dunes: the lidar and the aerial photographs (2026-11-11).** Every 1996-2009 survey and the
1993-2008 photographs of GIS 10-15, the coast unrolled along NC-12 (`dune-collision/1-observations/8-dune-change-by-survey/
HAT_dune_change_by_survey.py buxton`, until 2026-11-12 `8-buxton-dune-change/HAT_buxton_dune_change.py`; figures in
the workspace's `dune-collision/1-observations/8-dune-change-by-survey/`:
`buxton_dunes_by_survey.png`, `buxton_dune_change_between_surveys.png`, `buxton_dune_profiles.png`,
`buxton_aerial_by_year.png`). Every survey on the 2009 datum: the model's vertical offsets, then each survey's remaining
NC-12 difference from 2009 removed, so on the road at GIS 15 every survey is within 0.05 m of 2009
(`supporting/buxton_datum_check.csv`).

- **The original foredune at GIS 10-12 was destroyed, not lowered.** Through 1998 a continuous dune ridge (4-7 m) stands
  80-110 m seaward of today's road, seaward of the old road; it is cut down and pushed landward in 1997-1999 (GIS 11
  already by 1998; the largest loss, all along GIS 10-15, from 1998 to 1999) and ends at about 3.6-4 m, close to the
  storm series' Dennis peak (3.65 m): overtopped or flattened rather than scarped. GIS 13 keeps its front dune (about
  6 m); GIS 14-15 hold, with their seaward faces trimmed.
- **NC-12 was relocated landward after Dennis.** About 3,500 ft just north of Buxton, in place in a photograph of
  16 March 2000 (NCDOT, NC 12 Buxton to Avon Feasibility Study, December 2015, section 2.1 and Figure 4; Hannah's
  management timeline, 1999). The road moved 47-78 m at GIS 10-12 (`road_relocation_1978_2008.csv`), less at GIS 9,
  13 and 14. The September 1999 lidar, flown just after Dennis, still has the old (washed-out) road; the February-March
  2000 survey is the first with the new one.
- **The photographs confirm the road and the dune.** 1993-1997: the paved road lies on the old alignment and the
  vegetated foredune stands seaward of it the whole length (1998 covers GIS 12-15 only). October 2002: the road is on
  the relocated line, and between it and the beach at GIS 10-12 there is bare sand where the old road and foredune
  were; GIS 13-15 keep their vegetated foredune. 2008: a new dune line just seaward of the relocated road at GIS 10-13.
- **A new dune was built beside the new road.** In the lidar, a raised line seaward of the new road at GIS 10-12 from
  2001 and a 6-7 m ridge 25-35 m seaward of it at GIS 10-13 by 2004; the 2009 crest reading at GIS 10-12 is this dune.
- **What it means for the model.** At GIS 10-12 the lidar crest record is two dunes: the original foredune until 1999,
  then a constructed one after 2001. The model has one dune line, the historical road relocations switched off, and no
  dune construction with the relocation, so it can follow neither the loss (which also needs water levels high
  enough to overtop the dunes) nor the rebuild. No toe or Cs can fix these domains; their crest misfit is a known
  limitation, with the local water levels and the 1999 relocation and dune build as what would.
- Imagery gaps: 2005 lidar and 1998 photographs miss GIS 10-11; no 2009 Google Earth capture reaches GIS 10-15. The 2002
  frames come from the USGS draft delivery's zips (unzipped into the figure folder's `supporting/`, the originals
  untouched). 2001 is unclassified lidar.

## 5. The road manager's first-year bulldozing (found and fixed 2026-11-09)

**Why.** GIS 12-13 rose about 1 m in the first model year with the module off. **What.** A two-year diagnostic run
recording the dunes after each step. **Found.** `RoadwayManager`'s first bulldozing cleared the starting topography
above road grade (not overwash) onto the dune: up to 17 m3/m and 0.7 m at GIS 13, in every one of the 55 managed
domains (year-one sand against year-one crest jump r 0.93); alongshore diffusion added about 0.3 m a year at GIS 13
in the first two years. **Fix.** The first bulldozing grades the road but leaves the dunes alone
(`cascade/roadway_manager.py`, `_discard_first_bulldoze`, True by default since 2026-11-09). **Effect** (off / on, Cs
1e-5, Automorph toes): crest RMSE 0.96 to 0.89 / 0.93 to 0.87 m; GIS 12-13 2.42 to 1.87 / 2.33 to 1.78 m; the year-one
jump at GIS 13 0.67 to 0.07 m; shoreline RMSE 1.18 to 1.20 / 1.19 to 1.20 m/yr; island overwash about 8% more. Every
run before 2026-11-09 has the old step. Figures: `6-other-tests/road_first_bulldoze_fix_*`; the sweeps made before the
fix are kept in `3-cs-and-toes/before-road-fix-2026-11-09/`.

## 8. The two dune rows (2026-11-10)

Barrier3D keeps two cross-shore dune rows per cell; they start equal and drift apart, because Barrier3D grows the back
row at a third of the front row's rate (decay coefficient 3). Front minus back, median: +0.04 m after a year, +0.59 m
by 2009 with the module off (+0.40 m on), the front row higher in 79% of cells; up to 4-6 m apart where a storm lowered
the front row (not checked which process). The crest scored and the collision lowering both use the higher row.

## 9. The 2009 lidar datum (2026-11-09)

The 2009 lidar is the reference every survey is aligned to on NC-12: the model's 2009 DEM equals it (0.00 m at about
41,000 land points), the unshifted 1998, 2004 and 2005 surveys agree on the road within 0.02 m, and its metadata gives
NAVD88 (Geoid03), better than 0.20 m (1 sigma). The 2012-2020 surveys read 0.09-0.21 m higher before their shifts, so
2009 may be up to about 0.1 m low in absolute terms, inside its stated accuracy; no correction. The +0.37 m between the
transect and model-cell crests is the method (the highest point of a 1 m profile against a 10 m cell's centre), not
the datum.

## 10. New module options (2026-11-10 to 11)

All in `cascade/dune_collision.py`, the default unchanged (every earlier run reproduces), through
`Cascade(dune_collision_rows=...)` and `COLLISION_ROWS` in `HAT_dune_collision.py`; unit tests
`tests/test_dune_collision.py`, 56 passing.

- **Row modes** (`rows`): "proportional" (the default, the crest lowered by the volume over the 20 m dune and both rows
  in proportion), "front" (all from the seaward row, over its 10 m), "equal" (both rows by the same height).
- **Lateral erosion** (`rows="lateral"`, Laura's suggestion): the face retreats through the front row, which keeps its
  height until the sand it holds above the toe ((crest - toe) x 10 m) is gone, then drops to the toe; what is left
  goes on to the back row. The volume lost is kept per row as a deficit, refilled first by dune growth, and moved with
  the rows when Barrier3D migrates the dune line. A row is removed only by a storm that erodes it. The eroded sand is
  deleted, as in the other modes.
- **Tracking**, in Barrier3D's layout (per model year and cell, Barrier3D units): `collision_volume_TS`,
  `collision_storms_TS`, `dune_row_loss_TS` (rows eroded through to the toe: dune cell collapse) and, lateral,
  `collision_deficit_TS`. Each hindcast run writes them per domain and year to `logs/collision_cells_<variant>.csv`
  and its `tables/collision_cells.csv`; `HAT_dune_collision.py collapse <variant>` draws them.

  5,246 at Cs 1e-5, 17,672 at 2.5e-4. The recounted tables are in `3-cs-and-toes/logs/`.

## 12. The 1999 road relocation switched on (2026-11-11)

Script: `3-on-vs-off/HAT_road_relocation_test.py`; runs `cal_step2_growth_noscrape_reloc`, `cal_toeberm_cs1_reloc`,
`cal_toeberm_cs2_reloc` (HAT_dune_collision.py `RELOCATION_1999`: the base runs with `HAT_RELOCATIONS=1`, nothing else

- What the model's relocation does: in 1999 the road's setback grows by the measured displacement (GIS 9-14: 40->60,
  20->70, 10->90, 20->90, 20->70, 60->80 m). It removes no dune and builds none; the road only rebuilds dunes where it
  is under 20 m behind them, which before the move was GIS 11 alone.
- Effect: only GIS 10-11 change. At GIS 11 the road no longer manages the dune (1-2 rebuilds without the move, none
  with it): the crest stays higher through 2003-2008 (no drop after Isabel), then falls in 2008-2009 and is not
  rebuilt, ending 1.4-1.6 m lower (about 3 m against the lidar's 5.2 m); overwash rises and the shoreline retreats
  0.3-0.8 m/yr faster. GIS 10 moves -0.5 to +0.5 m. GIS 9 and 12-15 are
  unchanged to within 0.1 m.
- Fit: slightly worse. Crest RMSE GIS 9-15 1.55 -> 1.62 m (off), 1.46 -> 1.53 (Cs 1e-6), 1.36 -> 1.42 (Cs 2e-6); island
  RMSE +0.01 m; overwash false alarms +1 point.
- So moving the road does not give the 1998-2001 dune loss north of Buxton: in the lidar the dunes at GIS 10-12 are
  lost before the move (Bonnie and Dennis), and what stands from 2001-2004 is a constructed dune (section 4). To match
  it the model would need the dunes themselves prescribed in 1999-2000 (the lidar crest after Dennis, then the
  constructed dune), not the road.

## 13. When and where the module misses the measured crest drops (2026-11-11)

Script: `3-on-vs-off/HAT_collision_missed_drops.py`; figure `3-on-vs-off/1-module-on-off/missed_crest_drops.png`, pairs in

- The module catches 4 of the 55 measured drops (model at least half the drop); the module-off run also catches 4,

- When: 1998-99 (Bonnie, Dennis) is the largest miss, 15 domains losing about 1 m (GIS 10-12, 26, 53, 68-69, 81-88)

- Where (net 1996-2009, lidar against model): GIS 76 -4.3 against +0.3 m, 52 -3.3 against +0.3, 7 and 12 about -2

## 17. Background erosion in the missed crest-drop hotspots (2026-11-12, Laura's test)

Can a little more background (alongshore) erosion narrow the beach enough for storms to overtop and cut the tall
dunes? First on the section 16 base, face retreat, low back row, Cs 3e-6 (`cal_toeberm_lat_cs3_maxmed`).
`3-on-vs-off/HAT_be_hotspot_zones.py` (zones; `results` for the runs), `HAT_collision_on_off_position.py behot`;
figures and tables in `3-on-vs-off/6-other-tests/hotspot-erosion/` (`hotspot_erosion_<design>_*.png`, one set per
design, and `hotspot_erosion_designs_compared.png`).

- Hotspots: runs of at least 3 domains (one gap allowed) with missed lidar crest drops (drop of at least 0.5 m, the
  model dropping less than half; as section 13) on the base run: GIS 6-14, 23-27, 35-38, 51-55, 82-88.
- Against the source/sink zone rules applied to the base run's residual (smoothed CoastSat LRR minus the model's,
  |residual| > 0.5 m/yr over at least 3 domains of one sign, GIS 1, 5-7, 90 excluded): three hotspots lie on a
  sink, where the model erodes too little: Buxton GIS 10-14, Avon south 21-24, Rodanthe 82-89. GIS 35-38 and 51-55
  lie on sources (the model already erodes too much there), so added erosion would worsen the shoreline; left out.
- The 100% rate per domain is that residual, fixed from the base (not re-solved, so the overwash it sets off is a
  separate term): -0.50 to -2.00 m/yr; runs `cal_toeberm_lat_cs3_maxmed_be{25,50,75,100}` (`BE_HOTSPOT` in
  HAT_dune_collision.py).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0% | 0 | 0 | -0.95 | 0 | 1 / 23 | 9 | 1.26 m/yr | 31% |
| 25% | 0.24 | 0.36 | -0.59 | +3 | 3 / 21 | 9 | 1.21 | 31% |
| 50% | 0.47 | 0.91 | -0.04 | +38 | 6 / 18 | 9 | 1.19 | 33% |
| 75% | 0.71 | 1.34 | +0.40 | +50 | 7 / 17 | 10 | 1.21 | 32% |
| 100% | 0.95 | 1.73 | +0.79 | +56 | 7 / 17 | 14 | 1.26 | 31% |

(The three zones together; the gap is CoastSat minus model, (-) the model erodes too little. Outside the zones
nothing changes by more than 0.04 m/yr.)

- 50% closes the shoreline gap in all three zones (Buxton +0.09, Avon south -0.24, Rodanthe -0.02 m/yr left) and
  gives the best island shoreline RMSE of the five (1.19 against 1.26 m/yr); 75% and 100% overshoot.
- The imposed rate comes out amplified: the zones retreat 1.4-2.2 times what is imposed. At Avon south the factor is
  1.40 at every fraction with no overwash at all (its 6.3 m dunes are never overtopped), so about 1.4 is the
  shoreline's own response to background erosion here, without the dunes. Above it is the overwash feedback: at
  Buxton the factor goes from 1.36 (25%) to 2.16 (50%) as overwash rises from -5 to +82 m³/m, so between 25% and 50%
  the beach narrows enough for the dunes to be overtopped. Rodanthe rises steadily (1.72 to 2.08) with overwash +10
  to +72 m³/m. Read that way, roughly a third of the 50% run's extra retreat at Buxton and Rodanthe is cross-shore
  (overwash), the rest alongshore.
- The tall dunes do get overtopped and cut: GIS 12 falls from 6.7 m to 4.4-5.2 m (lidar about 4.0-4.5 m), GIS 13 to
  4.4-5.1 m (lidar 4.4 m in 2009), GIS 84 and 86 drop in 2000-03 near the lidar's 1999-2001 lows. Caught drops rise
  from 1 to 6 (50%) and 7 (75-100%) of 24, two of them in 1998-99 (GIS 11 and 87 at 50%). But most come a year or
  more late (GIS 12 in 1999-2000 at 75-100%, not 1997-99), the 2001-04 lows at GIS 11 are still missed, and
  model-only drops rise from 9 to 14 at 100%.
- Avon south does not respond in its dunes at any fraction: the shoreline moves, but the dunes are never overtopped,
  and its 3 missed drops (2001-09) stay missed.
- Read: background erosion at about 50% of the gap fixes the shoreline in these zones and lets about a quarter of
  the missed drops through, mostly at Buxton and Rodanthe, through overwash rather than collision; it does not
  produce the 1997-99 timing. Not yet tried with the road rule and Rodanthe 90%, or on 2009-2025.

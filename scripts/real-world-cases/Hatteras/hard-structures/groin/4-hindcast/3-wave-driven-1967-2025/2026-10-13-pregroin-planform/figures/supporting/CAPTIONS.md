# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`continuous_gap_1967_2025.png`.** **Tests:** whether one continuous history of the corrected groin's strength reproduces the groin's whole life, 1967-2025, with no reset to the observed shoreline. **How to read:** (a) the change since 1967 in the gap across the Buxton groins (GIS 6 seaward of GIS 5): one model run per line from the 1967 wet/dry shoreline (GIS 2-12, joined to the 1996 offset elsewhere); teal squares, the wet/dry photo dates; teal circles, annual CoastSat, which measures a different line, placed on the photos by its mean offset over the years both have (2004 left out). (b) The strength each groin run uses, as the share of the arriving drift held back. Shading: storms before 1984 replay 1984-1999. Model: the 1996 topography, RSLR 0.004, fills on (2014, 2017, 2022). **Shows:** the strengths fitted window by window (purple), run as one history, drop the gap by about 25 m when the strength falls in 1996 and stay 30-40 m low (39 m error for 1996-2009): those fits describe how a reset fillet relaxes, not how strong the groin was. Fitted era by era on the continuous run (black), the groin ramps to 0.25 by 1978, holds 0.25 to 2009 and 0.2 to 2016, and is off from the 2017 fill; it follows the record to 17 m over 1967-2025 (8 m in the 1970-1977 build-up, 19 m for 1978-1995, 18 m for 1996-2009, 10 m for 2010-2016, 16 m after the fill). The maintained 0.25 is the minimum under three different pre-1984 storm sequences (continuous_followups.csv).

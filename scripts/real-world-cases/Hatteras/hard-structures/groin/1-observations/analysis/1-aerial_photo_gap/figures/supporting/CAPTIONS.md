# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`photo_gap_1_wetdry_lines.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this is the raw record the rest of the folder measures. **How to read:** UTM 18N, true scale, north up, ocean to the right. Each line is one wet/dry line digitized from a dated aerial photo, 25 surveys in 24 years from 1967 to 2023 (two in 1972), coloured by date (`wet_dry_shorelines_groin.geojson` in the 1967 rig's inputs, the file the position table is made from). Grey boxes: the model's GIS domains 2-12, each 500 m alongshore. Red: the four Buxton groin lines. (a) The whole stretch the table covers; dashed box: the extent of (b). (b) The groins close up. **Shows:** the coast retreats landward (left) through time almost everywhere, and the lines fan out most just south of the groins, where the downdrift side lost the most ground.

**`photo_gap_2_measuring_position.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this shows how `HAT_geometric_distance_sanity_check.py` turns a digitized line into the per-domain numbers in `Change_from_wetdry_1967_D2_D12.csv`. **How to read:** (a) The October 1996 wet/dry line over GIS 4-7 at true scale, north up; each domain's clipped piece is drawn heavy (blue GIS 6, gold GIS 5) with its 25 sample points; teal arrows (every fourth point) run east toward the offshore datum line, which lies about 7.4 km offshore, off the panel; the label is the mean distance for that domain. (b) The five steps, and this survey's numbers for the two domains either side of the groins. **Shows:** a domain's position is one number per survey, the mean east-west distance from its piece of line to a fixed line offshore, and the table holds that number's change since 1967 (stored landward positive; the figures here flip it to seaward positive). The datum runs due north-south, so the distance is measured due east rather than straight offshore: the shoreline at GIS 5-6 runs about 21° from north, so an east-west change is about 1.07 times the shore-normal change. That scales both sides about alike and does not move the gap's timing.

**`photo_gap_3_positions_by_domain.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this is the position table every hindcast fit reads, drawn whole. **How to read:** each cell is one domain (rows, north at the top) in one survey (columns, in date order), the change in its mean distance to the datum since the 1967 survey (figure 2), seaward positive (blue) or landward (red), in metres; blank: that survey's line does not reach the domain. The red line is the GIS 5/6 boundary, where the southernmost groin sits (the field lies just inside GIS 6), so GIS 5 is the downdrift side and GIS 6 the updrift side. Source: `Change_from_wetdry_1967_D2_D12.csv`, sign flipped. **Shows:** the whole stretch retreats, but not evenly: GIS 4-5, just south of the groins, lose far more ground than GIS 6-8 just north of them, and the step in colour across the red line is the groin's effect. The next figure takes those two rows, GIS 5 and GIS 6, and their difference is the gap.

**`photo_gap_4_two_sides.png`.** **Tests:** nothing; this is the photo record of the groin's effect, read on its own. **How to read:** 24 survey years of dated aerial-photo wet/dry lines, 1967-2023 (two surveys in 1972, averaged). (a) Each side's position as change since 1967, seaward positive: the mean distance from the wet/dry line to a fixed offshore datum line over the GIS domain, from `Change_from_wetdry_1967_D2_D12.csv` with its sign flipped. (b) The gap, north minus south: rising means the north side is holding while the south side retreats. Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events (`../../data/structure_history.md`); orange: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. **Shows:** both sides retreated; the gap is the north side retreating less. It grew fast after the groins were built, peaked at 155 m in 1995 (partly the south side's loss to Gordon), stood at 135 m in 1996 and fell to 74 m by 2023, most of that fall around the 2017 fill and the losses. A photo is one day, so single surveys (1995, 2004) can sit well off the trend.

**`photo_gap_5_stage_rates.png`.** **Tests:** how fast the gap widened or narrowed in each stage of the groin's life, from the photos alone. **How to read:** (a) The photo gap (north minus south, seaward positive) with a least-squares line through the surveys in each stage: building (1967-1978), slow growth to the 1995 peak (1978-1995), and after the last repair to the year before the fill (1996-2016). (b) Each line's slope ± 1 standard error, with the number of survey years behind it. Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events. **Shows:** the gap built at +11.2 m/yr in the first decade, slowed to +1.6 ± 1.3 m/yr up to the peak, and narrowed at -1.4 ± 0.6 m/yr after the last repair. The photos are sparse after 1997 (one survey between 1997 and 2008, in 2004), so the timing of the turn is better read from CoastSat (`../2-coastsat_gap/`).

**`photo_gap_6_placement.png`.** **Tests:** whether the observed pattern of building out and retreat puts the groin's effect at the GIS 5/6 boundary, where the model groin sits, or at 6/7. **How to read:** (a) Each line is one survey's change since 1967 along the coast, measured as the position table is (mean distance to the offshore datum line) but in 100 m strips of northing instead of 500 m domains, seaward positive; x is northing from the southern groin. Dashed lines: the two candidate boundaries; red triangles: the groins (the two northern ones nearly coincide). (b) The gap across each boundary from the domain table: the north domain's change minus the south domain's. Dotted lines: the structure's recorded events; orange: the structural losses reported for 2015-2021. **Shows:** the shoreline does not step at one place. Through the groins' working years it rises steadily across the field, from the deepest retreat in GIS 4-5 through zero near 170 m (inside GIS 6, between the groins) to the most building-out at 470 m (1989), at the 6/7 boundary just north of the northern groins, then falls off through GIS 7 to near zero in GIS 8. So the starved side begins at the southern groin and the fillet sits against the northern groins: one domain-boundary groin can catch only one of the two. Across 5/6 the gap is 97-155 m over 1978-1997; across 6/7 it is 14-44 m, smaller because GIS 6's mean averages the ramp inside the field.

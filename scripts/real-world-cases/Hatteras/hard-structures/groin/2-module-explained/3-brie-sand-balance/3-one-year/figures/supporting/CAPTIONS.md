# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`brie_sand_headland_one_year.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; one year of an unmodified `brie.Brie` (alongshore step only, option A waves, 500 m sections) at a 100 m headland three sections wide, shown in plan view with north to the right (a sketch, not to scale). **How to read:** under the shoreline, each section's rate, which BRIE sets from the angle of the boundary on that section's north side (brie.py 820-826, 1294-1309): only the two sections whose north boundary is a headland edge get one; flat boundaries face straight into these waves, where BRIE's rate is zero. The bubbles give what each section books for the handover across the headland's two edges in one year (2 r Δ, BRIE's own numbers). **Shows:** at the south edge section 14 books sand arriving but the headland's first section, rate 0, books nothing leaving, so it appears; at the north edge section 17 books sand leaving and section 18 books nothing arriving, so it vanishes. Over BRIE's full year the island loses 15.0 m of shoreline (× 500 m × 19.8 m active depth = about 148,448 m³).

**`brie_sand_one_boundary_books.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains the bookkeeping in BRIE's alongshore step at one boundary. **How to read:** (a) the rule. Sand crossing a boundary should leave one domain and arrive in the next in the same amount. BRIE computes each domain's angle from the boundary to its north neighbour (brie.py 820-826), turns it into one rate r from its wave-climate diffusivity table (1294-1309), and applies that rate to both of the domain's boundaries (1311-1324). (b) a real Brie (option A waves) started from a 100 m seaward step three domains wide. At each edge of the step, the domain to the south books 2 r_south Δ across the boundary and the domain to the north books −2 r_north Δ, where Δ is the step across the boundary and 2 r Δ is the first-order amount in one year of BRIE's Crank-Nicolson step. The domain whose north boundary is the step gets a rate from the step's angle (up to 113.9 per 1,000), the domain beyond it gets the rate of a flat boundary, which under option A is zero (BRIE clips negative diffusivity to zero), so the books disagree. (c) the same step with one rate per boundary: both sides use it and the books agree. **Shows:** BRIE's sand budget balances wherever neighbouring boundaries have the same angle; at a bend it does not, because the two domains disagree about how much crossed the boundary between them.

# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`brie_sand_both_sides_one_rate.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure answers the objection that BRIE cannot make or lose sand because its step looks at both sides of each section. **How to read:** top, BRIE's update for one section (brie.py 1311-1324): the shoreline positions come from both neighbours (blue), the rate r from the angle of the section's north boundary only (gold; brie.py 820-826, 1294-1309) and is applied to both sides. Middle, the boundary at the south edge of a 100 m headland in one year of an unmodified `brie.Brie` (alongshore step only, option A waves): with one rate for both sections their books agree; with BRIE's own rates, section 14 (whose north boundary is the headland's edge) books 3.7 m arriving and section 15 (whose north boundary is flat, rate 0 under these waves) books nothing leaving. Bottom, the sum over the island. **Shows:** the objection is right about the positions and, for a single rate, about conservation; the error comes from each section having its own rate, so it appears only where neighbouring rates differ, at a bend.

**`brie_sand_equation_worked_example.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; BRIE's alongshore step worked through by hand on six sections, every number from a real `brie.Brie` (barrier and inlet models off, option A waves, 500 m sections). **How to read:** left to right, the three lines of BRIE's step and the bookkeeping they imply. (1) each section's angle comes from x[i+1] − x[i] only: `x_s[np.r_[1:ny, 0]]` is the list shifted by one, the next section to the north. (2) the angle picks one diffusivity from BRIE's wave-climate table, and BRIE sets negative ones to zero. (3) the update uses the positions on both sides but that one rate. (4) at each boundary the two sections' books do not cancel where their rates differ. **Shows:** where the angle, and so the rate, comes from, and why the positions on both sides do not stop sand being made or lost.

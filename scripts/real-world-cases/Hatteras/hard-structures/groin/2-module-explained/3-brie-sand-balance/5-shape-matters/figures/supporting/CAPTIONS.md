# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`brie_sand_where_it_matters.png`.** **Tests:** whether unmodified BRIE keeps the island's sand constant over 50 years on three coasts (alongshore step only, option A waves, 500 m sections, nothing added or removed). **How to read:** each coast as a sketch, with the change in the island's summed shoreline after 50 years and the volume it stands for (× 500 m × BRIE's active depth). **Shows:** BRIE's sand budget holds on a straight coast and nearly on a gentle bend; at a sharp bend it does not. This is why colleagues who use BRIE on smooth coasts see it conserve sand, and why a groin, which makes a sharp bend, exposes the error.

**`brie_sand_how_sharp.png`.** **Tests:** how the sand BRIE's step makes or loses depends on how sharply the coast bends. **How to read:** each point is one year of an unmodified `brie.Brie` (alongshore step only, option A waves) started from a step three domains wide, of height −200 to +200 m (seaward +), plotted against the angle the coast turns at the step's edges. The shaded band is ±5°. **Shows:** a straight coast conserves exactly and a gently turning one nearly; the error grows roughly with the square of the turn. On this coast, facing the waves, a step either way loses sand; on other shapes and orientations the error can make sand instead (the groin-like fillet in brie_sand_four_shapes gains, and the fixed-shift groin made +539 m in 50 years at −20°). A groin's fillet or a sharp natural bend is where it matters.

**`brie_sand_four_shapes.png`.** **Tests:** whether BRIE's alongshore step keeps the island's sand constant on four coastline shapes, with an unmodified `brie.Brie` (barrier and inlet models off, so only the alongshore step moves the shoreline; option A waves; 30 domains of 500 m; nothing added or removed; 40 years). **How to read:** top, each shape at the start (dashed) and after 40 years, BRIE as written (black) and with one rate per boundary (green). Middle, year 1 at every boundary: what the domain to the south books across it minus what the domain to the north books (first order, 2 r Δ each); gold is sand made, red sand lost, and an empty boundary is one where the two agree. Bottom, the island's summed shoreline against its start (green: one rate per boundary, zero throughout). **Shows:** wherever the coast bends, the two domains at a boundary use different rates and the books disagree; along straight stretches they agree. Over the years, the groin-like fillet gains 8.8 m, the sharp headland loses 105.8 m, the gentle bump loses 9.0 m, the bay loses 51.3 m. With one rate per boundary all four conserve to round-off.

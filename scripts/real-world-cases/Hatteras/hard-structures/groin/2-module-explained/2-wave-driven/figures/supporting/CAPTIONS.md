# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`wave_driven_2_explained.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains how the wave-driven groin (the corrected trapping groin, straight-coast module 6) works. **How to read:** (a) a plan-view sketch, not to scale: land above, ocean below, the updrift side on the right, the dashed line the coast before the groin. Each year: (1) the waves bring the net longshore drift toward the groin; (2) the groin holds back 60% of it, moving that sand from the cell just downdrift to the cell just updrift; (3) BRIE's alongshore diffusion does not know the groin is there and carries sand back across the groin face, more as the step across it grows; (4) a correction returns what BRIE's solve creates or destroys near the groin, so total sand is conserved. (b) and (c): the model, one groin on a straight coast at -20 degrees, option A waves. (b) The sand held back and the sand carried back each year, as metres of shoreline in one 500 m cell (the carried-back flow is approximate: the groin face's diffusion number times the step). (c) The gap between the updrift and downdrift cells. **Shows:** the groin holds back nearly the same every year (57 m in year 1, 57.6 m in year 60), while what BRIE carries back grows with the gap (15 m in year 1, 38 m in year 60); the rest spreads along the coast. The gap therefore grows fast at first (104 m after year 1) and slowly later (206 m at year 30, 215 m at year 60).

**`wave_driven_3_steps.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure shows where the wave-driven groin (the runner's groin since 2026-10-13) acts in a CASCADE model year, and the shape it builds, starting from a straight coast; the same layout as `../1-fixed-shift/figures/fixed_shift_3_steps.png`. **How to read:** (a) the order within one call of `Cascade.update()`: Barrier3D in every domain (storms, overwash, dunes, background erosion), then the groin hook (`_groin_callback`), then BRIE's alongshore transport, then the management modules; then the next year. (b)-(e) the first year of the straight-coast run, from a straight coast at -20 degrees (BRIE's alongshore step alone, option A waves): the shoreline at the start, after Barrier3D (unchanged: the test has none; at Buxton storms and background erosion act here), after the groin's edit (arrows), and after BRIE smooths it alongshore (dashed: the shoreline before each step). (f) the same year repeated for 50 years, on its own scale (dashed: the straight start). Red: the groin, the face between GIS 5 and GIS 6. **Shows:** the groin holds back 60% of the drift arriving: in year 1, 58 m of shoreline moved from GIS 5 to GIS 6, more than the fixed-shift groin's fixed 12 m, because it is read from the waves. Its second part, cancelling BRIE's leak near the groin, is zero in year 1 (the coast is still straight, so there is no bend to leak at) and grows as the step does. BRIE then spreads the notch and bump, and after 50 years the shape is a fillet north of the groin and an equal cut south of it, with +4 m of sand made: balanced, where the fixed-shift groin made 539 m on the same coast.

**`wave_driven_1_in_the_model_year.png`.** **Tests:** nothing new; this figure explains where the wave-driven groin (`GroinCallback`, the runner's groin since 2026-10-13) acts in a CASCADE model year. The companion of `../1-fixed-shift/figures/fixed_shift_1_in_the_model_year.png`. **How to read:** (a) the order within one year: every Barrier3D domain runs first, including its background erosion (BE), and hands BRIE its shoreline change for the year; the groin then edits that change, in the same place the fixed-shift groin did (`_groin_callback` on the sandbox Cascade); BRIE then moves the shoreline alongshore, unchanged. (b) the edit in year 10 of the straight-coast run with the coast at -20° (BRIE's alongshore step alone, option A waves, strength 0.6), drawn on GIS labels as if it were Buxton: grey, a uniform 1.5 m of retreat from Barrier3D in every domain (made up for the example); gold, the drift held back, 56.7 m moved from GIS 5 to GIS 6, read from BRIE's own drift at the angle of the face the drift arrives on; blue, the leak correction near the groin, which sums to -27.7 m and cancels the +27.7 m BRIE's step would otherwise make at the groin's bends (zero in year 1, when there are no bends yet); dark, what BRIE receives. At Buxton the strength is 0.16 (1996-2009) or 0.10 (2009-2017), so the amounts are smaller. **Shows:** unlike the fixed-shift groin's 12 m, the amount held back follows the waves, and the correction keeps the island's sand constant, so the groin moves sand but never makes or loses it.

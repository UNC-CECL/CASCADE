# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`fine_reference_1_gap_and_profiles.png`.** **Tests:** whether each version of the groin module, with one groin on BRIE's 500 m grid, reproduces what a real-sized groin field does to the two domains either side of it: the gap between them over time, and the shoreline after 50 years. **How to read:** the reference is a one-line model on 25 m cells with the same wave-climate drift as BRIE, and three groins 200 m apart inside the updrift domain, as at Buxton; each groin first holds 0.6 of the drift and lets more sand round its tip as the fillet reaches it (GENESIS-style bypassing). Broad grey: the reference with 110 m groins, averaged to domains; dashed grey: the same with 55 and 220 m groins; dotted grey in (d-f): the reference on its own 25 m cells. Coloured: the four modules. (a-c) the updrift domain minus the downdrift domain, the quantity measured at Buxton. (d-f) shoreline change, seaward positive, updrift side on the right. **Shows:** the reference field fills to its tips within 5 to 20 years at -30 to +20 degrees and then holds a gap of about twice the groin length (113, 227 and 461 m at -20 degrees for the three lengths); at +30 degrees, where the drift is a tenth of its grid-aligned value, it is still filling at year 50. No module has a groin length, so none levels off at a length. At -20 degrees the wave-driven groin ends at 214 m against the reference's 227 m; at +20 degrees it holds only 44 m, the drift version 371 m, and the pinned version builds its gap on the wrong side (-230 m) (tables/).

**`fine_reference_2_errors.png`.** **Tests:** across shoreline orientations from -30 to +30 degrees, how far each version of the groin module, on BRIE's 500 m grid, sits from the 25 m reference field with 110 m groins. **How to read:** (a) the root-mean-square difference in the gap between the updrift and downdrift domains over years 1 to 50; (b) the same for the shoreline after 50 years, over the 22 domains around the groin. Lower is closer. **Shows:** averaged over the seven orientations, the gap error is 71 m for the wave-driven groin, 103 m drift, 116 m dipole and 241 m pinned; the shoreline error 25, 121, 51 and 67 m. The corrected version is closest on coasts turned against the drift and drifts away from the reference on coasts turned with it, where the reference still fills to its tips and the module traps little (tables/fine_reference_scores.csv).

**`fine_reference_3_length.png`.** **Tests:** whether the wave-driven groin with a groin length (module 7) makes the gap across the groin scale with the groin's length the way a real-sized field does. **How to read:** the gap between the updrift and downdrift domains after 50 years against the groin length L, for the 25 m reference (solid, circles) and module 7 on BRIE's 500 m grid with the same L (dashed, pink triangles), one shade per orientation from -30 (light) to +30 degrees (dark); the dotted red line is a gap of twice the groin length. **Shows:** the reference follows gap = 2L at -30 to +20 degrees (108 to 461 m). Module 7 rises far more slowly: from 80-101 m at 55 m to 159-246 m at 220 m at -30 to +10 degrees, and near zero at +20 and +30. Its blocking reads the build-out of the whole 500 m updrift cell, which BRIE's diffusion keeps well short of the tip, so the groin starts passing sand long before a real one would (tables/fine_reference_length_check.csv).

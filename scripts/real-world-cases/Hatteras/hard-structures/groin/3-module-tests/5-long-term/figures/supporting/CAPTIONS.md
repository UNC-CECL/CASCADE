# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`long_term_1_gap_over_time.png`.** **Tests:** whether each version of the groin module holds a steady gap across the groin over 200 years, or keeps growing, collapses or runs away, and whether it keeps tracking a real-sized groin field. **How to read:** one groin on a straight coast at seven orientations, BRIE's alongshore solve alone (option A waves; no storms, no Barrier3D, no sea-level rise), 61 domains. Lines: the gap between the updrift and downdrift domains, the quantity measured at Buxton. Broad grey: the 25 m reference with three 110 m groins, averaged to domains. **Shows:** see the README beside this figure for the numbers; at -20 degrees the wave-driven groin's gap is 219 m at year 200 against the reference's 223 m (tables/).

**`long_term_2_end_state.png`.** **Tests:** at the end of a 200-year run, whether each version of the groin module still conserves sand, has stopped growing, and has kept the shoreline within the angles BRIE can handle. **How to read:** against the shoreline orientation: (a) the net change in sand over the coast at the last year, as summed shoreline (symmetric log scale; zero is conserved); (b) the change in the gap over the last 50 years (near zero is levelled off); (c) the steepest shoreline angle across any face in any year (BRIE's diffusivity table ends at 90 degrees, where a runaway stops the model). Grey: the 25 m reference. **Shows:** the numbers are in tables/long_term_summary.csv and the README beside this figure.

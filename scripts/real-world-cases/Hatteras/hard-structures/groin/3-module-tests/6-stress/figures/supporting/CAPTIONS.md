# Captions — figures

Written by the figure scripts through `hat_figure_style.caption()`; the images carry no titles or footnotes, this file does.

**`stress_1_where_it_breaks.png`.** **Tests:** whether the runner's groin (`cascade.groin.GroinManager`, unchanged, 10-cell correction zone) keeps the sand balance and stays stable when one wave parameter at a time is moved far from option A (Hs 2.0, Tp 7.5, asymmetry 0.6, high-angle fraction 0.5). **How to read:** BRIE's alongshore step alone on a straight coast, one groin, 100 years; each cell is one run, scored against the same run with the groin off. Colour: the sand the groin made (blue) or lost (red) by year 100, summed over the coast, on a log scale that is linear inside ±5 m. Letters: the first rule broken. X the run crashed (an angle past BRIE's diffusivity table); c sand not conserved (more than 5 m or 5% of the groin's gross change); a a face steeper than 60$^\circ$; s the gap still moving over the last fifth of the run. Grey-ruled rows are option A. Buxton's groin cells start at about −12$^\circ$ and −25$^\circ$. **Shows:** at option A the groin holds the balance at every orientation but +10$^\circ$ (+34 m at strength 0.25). It breaks two ways: where BRIE's diffusion is strong (high-angle fraction 0.3 or less, Hs 2.5 and more on coasts turned with the drift) the leak correction overshoots and runs away, and where BRIE's diffusivity is clipped to zero at the groin (asymmetry 0.7 and more on coasts turned against the drift) nothing carries sand back across the face, so the gap grows without limit.

**`stress_2_other_stresses.png`.** **Tests:** at option A waves, whether the runner's groin keeps the sand balance and stays stable under the other stresses: its strength from 0 to 1; a sharp step already at the groin when it starts (the gap in metres, updrift seaward positive; Buxton's is 256 m); on for 50 years then off for 100; on and off every 5 years; cross-shore change passed in as Barrier3D passes it (2 m/yr retreat, ±10 m/yr noise per domain, a 30 m cut every 10 years); the groin face near the end of the array (lo is the lower domain of the face, of 61); 500 years; and a new wave climate every year (asymmetry 0.4-0.8, Hs 1.5-2.5, the groin as written and with its drift table refreshed each year; mean of three seeds, three orientations). **How to read:** as figure 1: colour is the net sand against the matched no-groin run, letters the first rule broken. **Shows:** strength 0 is the no-groin run exactly, and up to 0.5 the groin conserves at every orientation; the switch-off, the background change and 500 years leave it conserving at strength 0.25 except at +10$^\circ$; it leaks when switched on and off every 5 years, when its face is within 10 cells of the end of the array (up to 1528 m: the correction zone is cut off there and does not wrap), and in almost every run where the waves change from year to year.

**`stress_3_two_failure_modes.png`.** **Tests:** how the runner's groin fails when it does, year by year. **How to read:** BRIE's alongshore step alone, a straight coast at -20$^\circ$ (Buxton's groin cells are at about −12$^\circ$ and −25$^\circ$), strength 0.25, against the same run with no groin. Top: the gap across the groin (updrift seaward of downdrift); bottom: the sand the groin made or lost, summed over the coast; both on log scales that are linear near zero (the top right is linear). Grey is option A in each column. (a, c) Lower high-angle fraction makes BRIE's diffusion stronger; at 0.3 and 0.1 the leak correction, which is an explicit term in front of BRIE's implicit step, overshoots and grows each year; at 0.1 the run ends where an angle runs off BRIE's diffusivity table. (b, d) Higher asymmetry clips BRIE's diffusivity to zero at the groin's angles; nothing carries sand back across the face, the groin keeps moving its share of the drift every year, and the gap grows without limit. **Shows:** neither happens at option A; both happen inside the range of the groin-on wave sweep (high-angle 0.3, asymmetry 0.7 and 0.8).
